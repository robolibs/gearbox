# Mars and Lunar Greenhouse and Habitat Designs, and the Robots That Tend Them (as of October 2026)

## Greenhouse and habitat architectures proposed or built for Mars and the Moon (dimensions, materials, growing area, shielding, pressure)

### Takeaway
The only fully specified space-analogue greenhouse is DLR's EDEN ISS, a 20 ft high-cube shipping container. It has an internal room of 5.7 x 2.15 x 2.55 m, a 1.0 m centre aisle and about 12.5 m² of growing area on shelves up to four levels high. It ran in Antarctica from 2018 to about 2022, returned to Bremen in 2023, and is being converted into EDEN LUNA at the ESA/DLR LUNA hall in Cologne, with a project term of 2024 to 2026. Every other concept (the Arizona inflatable cylinders, Mars Ice Home, MARSHA, Mars X-House, Lunar Palace 1, CHAPEA's Mars Dune Alpha) is a study, an analogue or a competition entry. Each gives only partial numbers. The details to reuse are a pressurised volume of 1 to 4 storeys, an Earth-like 101 kPa cabin, and shielding from regolith or water ice.

### Cited Findings

**EDEN ISS Mobile Test Facility (MTF), DLR. Built 2015–2017, Antarctic operations 2018 to ~2022, now EDEN LUNA**
- The MTF is two customised 20 ft high-cube shipping containers. Container 1 holds the Cold Porch, which acts as an airlock between outside and the work area, plus the Service Section (most subsystems, a work desk with an integrated sink, storage, and an ISS rack-like plant growth facility). Container 2 holds the Future Exploration Greenhouse (FEG), separated from the Service Section by a wall and a sealed door. The FEG is 50% of MTF volume. — [Zabel et al., ICES-2017-95, FEG Design](https://elib.dlr.de/114876/1/Zabel%20et%20al.%20FEG%20Design%20ICES%202017_v3.pdf)
- FEG internal space: 2150 mm wide x 2550 mm high x 5700 mm long, about 32 m³. The operational table gives 31.50 m³ internal volume, 12.26 m² floor area, ~12.5 m² total cultivation area and ~0.6 m² of cultivation area per level. — [Zabel et al. 2017](https://elib.dlr.de/114876/1/Zabel%20et%20al.%20FEG%20Design%20ICES%202017_v3.pdf)
- Construction: about 80 mm rock wool insulation and 10 mm wood plates on the inner walls, covered with white high-pressure laminate that is fire retardant and has an antimicrobial top layer. The floor has ~120 mm polyurethane foam. A stainless-steel basin 100 mm high covers the whole floor and catches leaks, which can be pumped to the wastewater tank. — [Zabel et al. 2017](https://elib.dlr.de/114876/1/Zabel%20et%20al.%20FEG%20Design%20ICES%202017_v3.pdf)
- Raised floor: anodised steel bars inside the basin. The sub-floor is 300 mm high and holds pumps, water pipes and cooling-fluid pipes. The corridor floor is metal grating so air can move into the sub-floor. — [Zabel et al. 2017](https://elib.dlr.de/114876/1/Zabel%20et%20al.%20FEG%20Design%20ICES%202017_v3.pdf)
- Layout: centre aisle with fixed shelves on both sides. The corridor is 1000 mm wide so that 2–3 operators can work at once. A side-aisle design was rejected because it would have needed ~1150 mm deep racks, too deep to reach. Movable racks were rejected to cut moving parts. The rack structure is 2250 mm high and the shelves are 575 mm deep on each side. There are four racks per side: the north wall is "left" (L1–L4) and the south wall is "right" (R1–R4). Racks have 1, 2 or 4 levels and are built from 30 x 30 mm aluminium profile, which also carries the lamps, pipes and pumps. — [Zabel et al. 2017](https://elib.dlr.de/114876/1/Zabel%20et%20al.%20FEG%20Design%20ICES%202017_v3.pdf)
- Atmosphere setpoints: O2 21%, CO2 750 ppm, pressure 101 kPa (set by outside conditions), 21 °C with lights on and 19 °C with lights off, 75% RH lit and 85% RH dark. — [Zabel et al. 2017](https://elib.dlr.de/114876/1/Zabel%20et%20al.%20FEG%20Design%20ICES%202017_v3.pdf). Later reported operating setpoints were 330–600 µmol/m²/s, 21 °C, ~65% RH, 1000 ppm CO2 and a 17 h photoperiod — [Frontiers in Plant Science 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpls.2020.00656/full)
- Location: on a raised platform 400 m south of Neumayer Station III (70°40′S, 008°16′W) on the Ekström ice shelf. — [Zabel et al., ICES-2019-138, Crewtime](https://elib.dlr.de/128530/1/Zabel_EDEN%20ISS%20crew%20time_v2.pdf)
- Status: EDEN ISS ran near Neumayer III from 2018 to about 2022/2023 and was shipped back to DLR Bremen in April 2023. It is being converted for the LUNA facility at DLR Cologne, with mission control at the EDEN MCC in Bremen. The EDEN LUNA project runs 1 Jan 2024 to 31 Dec 2026. LUNA opened on 25 Sep 2024. The greenhouse is planned to operate within LUNA until 2026, with expansion afterwards. Planned upgrades: cameras, AI-controlled plant management, urine processed into nutrient solution, staged algae reactors, food processing and waste management, and a robotic arm. — [DLR EDEN LUNA page](https://www.dlr.de/en/research-and-transfer/projects-and-missions/eden-luna)
- EDEN LUNA is "attached to the LUNA main hall". Its subsystems are NDS (nutrients), AMS (temperature and humidity), CROP (urine to nutrient solution), PCDS (power), DHCS (data), LCS (lighting intensity, spectrum and duration) and a thermal control system. — [Prince et al., IAC-24 EVE crew-time paper](https://elib.dlr.de/210298/1/IAC24_manuscript_Autonomous_Robotic_System_Plant_Cultivation_Luna_Surface_author_version.pdf)

**University of Arizona CEAC Prototype Mars-Lunar Greenhouse (MLGH/LGH). Prototype from the 2010s, NASA article 2017; no flight program found**
- Four inflatable hydroponic cylinders joined by a hallway, a full-scale version of the four greenhouses in a proposed inflatable lunar outpost. Each unit is 2.06 m in diameter and 5.5 m long. One module has 11 m² of horizontal canopy area and 21 m³ of internal volume. A datalogger monitors and controls the environment. — [UA CEAC facilities](https://ceac.arizona.edu/about/facilities)
- NASA KSC gives a different size: about 5.5 m (18 ft) long and "over 2.4 m (8 ft)" in diameter, built by Sadler Machine Co. Units would probably be buried under regolith for radiation protection, which would require specialised lighting. Lighting could be LED or hybrid natural/artificial, using sun-tracking concentrators that feed fibre-optic bundles. — [NASA, "Lunar, Martian Greenhouses Designed to Mimic Those on Earth" (2017)](https://www.nasa.gov/science-research/lunar-martian-greenhouses-designed-to-mimic-those-on-earth). Diameter conflict: CEAC says 2.06 m; press reports range from 7 ft 3 in to 8 ft — [UA CEAC](https://ceac.arizona.edu/about/facilities)

**Mars Ice Home, NASA Langley with SEArch+ and Clouds AO. Concept study 2016–2018; no hardware program found**
- An inflatable torus inside a shell of water-ice cells. The ice provides GCR shielding and still lets daylight in. Basic robotics pre-deploy it and fill it with water before the crew arrives. The water can later be converted to propellant for the ascent vehicle. — [Universe Today](https://www.universetoday.com/articles/nasa-might-build-ice-house-mars)
- Size: 12 m internal diameter, built from a torus 6 m in diameter split into two levels, giving about 198 m² (~2131 ft²) of floor. The habitation torus is ~6 m high with at least 3 m of ice above it, for a deployed height of ~10 m. The crew is 4. The system mass target is under 18,000 kg and it must fit an 8 m cargo shroud; the SLS cargo fairing is 8.4 m. — [Ice Home ConOps MIH.ConOps.001 Rev 1.20 (Dec 2017)](http://bigidea.nianet.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/IceDome-ConOps-2017-12-21v-reduced.pdf)
- Pressure: the habitat is assumed at 14.7 psi, with 10.2 psi considered as a compromise. Water cells are at 3 psi. — [ConOps](http://bigidea.nianet.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/IceDome-ConOps-2017-12-21v-reduced.pdf). A Technical Risk Reduction paper cites cells at 6895 Pa (1.0 psi) instead — [ResearchGate, Technical Risk Reduction for the Mars Ice Home](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/330832840_Technical_Risk_Reduction_for_the_Mars_Ice_Home_Habitat_Concept)
- Greenhouse use: the 2nd floor can be logistics storage or "an area to grow fresh food". A greenhouse derivative attached to an access port was also proposed. Linear soft-goods seals would separate the "dry cooler lower work area" from the "warmer humid upper area for plant growth". In the illustrated crew day, crew wake up to a view of the garden from their quarters. Life: a 550-day operational phase plus 2+ years of deployment while ISRU fills the cells. The fill rate needed is ~0.25 m³/day (~10 kg/h). — [ConOps](http://bigidea.nianet.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/IceDome-ConOps-2017-12-21v-reduced.pdf)

**NASA 3D-Printed Habitat Centennial Challenge. Concluded May 2019**
- Phase 3 Level 4 (virtual): 1st SEArch+/Apis Cor ($33,954.11), 2nd Zopherus, 3rd Mars Incubator, sharing $100,000. — [Architect Magazine](https://www.architectmagazine.com/technology/top-three-teams-awarded-100-000-in-nasas-3d-printed-habitat-challenge_o). Mars X-House is a dual-shell module of Martian materials with trough-shaped ports on the side and top to admit light. — [3D Printing Industry](https://3dprintingindustry.com/news/nasa-enters-final-stage-of-3d-printed-habitat-challenge-with-announcement-of-phase-3-winners-152409/). The Level 3 brief was a 1,000 ft² living space for a one-year mission. The head-to-head print was 1–4 May 2019 with an $800,000 purse. — [Architect Magazine](https://www.architectmagazine.com/technology/top-three-teams-awarded-100-000-in-nasas-3d-printed-habitat-challenge_o)
- AI SpaceFactory MARSHA (overall winner): "an 800 to 1000 square foot habitat, four stories tall" for 4 astronauts for up to 2 years, according to the company's David Malott. — [CBC Quirks & Quarks](https://amp.cbc.ca/radio/quirks/may-9-covid-stress-and-pregnancy-a-black-hole-in-our-backyard-and-more-1.5561112/how-to-build-habitats-on-mars-and-how-to-live-in-them-1.5561114). The material is basalt fibre from Martian rock plus PLA bioplastic made from Mars-grown plants. — [local21news / Sinclair](https://local21news.com/news/nation-world/3d-printed-home-may-house-future-astronauts-to-mars). Level plan per one description: L1 garage with external systems and wet lab; L2 34 m² dry lab and kitchen; **L3 cabins, sanitation pod and a hydroponic garden**; L4 recreation and exercise. — [livinspaces](https://livinspaces.net/ls-tv/ai-spacefactory-use-3d-printing-technology-to-develop-marsha-a-possible-habitat-for-mars/). CBC gives a different floor assignment: L1 airlocks and lab, L2 kitchen, L3 sleep pods and washroom, L4 recreation — [CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/radio/quirks/may-9-covid-stress-and-pregnancy-a-black-hole-in-our-backyard-and-more-1.5561112/how-to-build-habitats-on-mars-and-how-to-live-in-them-1.5561114). A report of "15 ft high, 8 ft diameter" pods printed in 30 h describes the competition-scale prints, not the full habitat — [local21news](https://local21news.com/news/nation-world/3d-printed-home-may-house-future-astronauts-to-mars)

**NASA CHAPEA / Mars Dune Alpha (ICON 3D-printed, JSC Houston). Active**
- 1,700 ft² interior with four crew quarters. A 1,200 ft² red-sand "sandbox" outside is for simulated Marswalks. — [Space.com tour](https://www.space.com/nasa-mock-mars-dune-alpha-chapea-habitat-video-tour). The design-stage plan for the crop area was "four large tray towers for aeroponic crop growth" next to the kitchen and near trash disposal — [ASCE Civil Engineering, Sep 2023](https://asce.org/publications-and-news/civil-engineering-source/civil-engineering-magazine/issues/magazine-issue/article/2023/09/a-3d-printed-habitat-is-home-for-yearlong-missions-to-mars). The habitat has dedicated areas for crop growth among other functions — [NASA About CHAPEA](https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/chapea/about-chapea/)
- Mission 1 (2023–2024) crew grew tomatoes, peppers and greens to supplement shelf-stable food — [Live Science](https://livescience.com/space/mars/nasas-1st-year-long-mock-mars-mission-wraps-up-in-houston). Mission 2 began 19 Oct 2025 with crew Elder, Ellis, Montgomery and Spicer, who were reported to exit on 31 Oct 2026 after a year that included crop growing — [autoevolution](https://www.autoevolution.com/news/second-crew-of-mars-astronauts-nasa-locked-up-for-a-year-are-coming-out-on-october-31-277265.html)

**Lunar Palace 1 (Yuegong-1), Beihang University, Beijing. 105-day mission 2014; "Lunar Palace 365" 2017–2018**
- Two plant cabins plus one comprehensive (crew) cabin: 160 m² total, 500 m³. The plant cabins have **120 m² of total planting area in a "three-layer stereoscopic" design**, and each plant cabin is split into two parts with independent environmental control. Gas leakage was 0.043%/day. — [Fu/Liu et al., bioRxiv 2021](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.01.12.426282v1.full-text). Wikipedia gives a 58 m² vegetation area across two cabins and a 42 m² living area — [Wikipedia Yuegong-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuegong-1). China Daily, citing Beihang, gives 150 m² for the whole lab — [China Daily 2018](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/201805/16/WS5afb6af8a3103f6866ee8945_5.html)
- Cabin I uses solid media (vermiculite or soil-like substrate made from solid waste) under continuous 24/0 h light at 300–800 µmol/m²/s. Cabin II is hydroponic, 12/12 h, 250–800 µmol/m²/s. Both use Hoagland solution. There are 35 species: 5 staple crops, 29 vegetables and 1 fruit, with wheat taking the largest area. Measured conditions: ~22–23 °C and ~62% RH in the plant cabins; crew cabin 24 °C and 50% RH; CO2 averaged 1,241 ppm (range 246–4,131), with growth inhibited above ~3,000 ppm. — [bioRxiv 2021](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.01.12.426282v1.full-text)

**MDRS GreenHab, Mars Society, Utah. Operating analogue**
- The second-generation GreenHab (built 2003) was a horizontal cylinder in two sections: a larger vegetable section and a smaller flower "Zen garden". It connected to the Hab by a corridor and was reconfigured as a greenhouse in 2009. — [Wikipedia MDRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_Desert_Research_Station). It was destroyed by fire on 29 Dec 2014, caused by an electrical heater. — [Space.com](https://www.space.com/28388-greenhouse-fire-mock-mars-mission.html). The rebuild plan included hydroponics convertible to aquaponics, LEDs, environmental controls, fans and sensors, at a cost of $40k (target $10k with volunteers). — [Space.com 2015](https://space.com/30143-mars-society-greenhouse-crowdfunding-campaign.html). The replacement was planned as a geodesic dome — [HobbySpace](https://hobbyspace.com/Blog/?p=9549)
- An MDRS growth area of 5 m² with ~45 min/day of crew time is cited in — [Prince et al. IAC-24](https://elib.dlr.de/210298/1/IAC24_manuscript_Autonomous_Robotic_System_Plant_Cultivation_Luna_Surface_author_version.pdf)

**Fiction reference: "The Martian" Hab**
- Hab floor of 92 m². Watney planned 10 cm of soil over the floor, cut to 2/3 of the floor (62.5 m²) because water was short, and started with a 5 m² test planter. — [LitCharts ch. 2](https://www.litcharts.com/lit/the-martian/chapter-2). Using bunks and pop-tents, the farmable area was 126 m² — [Warwick](https://warwick.ac.uk/newsandevents/knowledgecentre/science/physics-astrophysics/growing_potatoes_on_mars)

**Historic NASA reference chambers (sizing anchors)**
- The KSC Biomass Production Chamber had a 20 m² growing area and 113 m³ volume. A Wheeler (2004) sizing slide gives ~1–5 m² total for short missions, ~10–25 m²/person for longer ones and ~50 m²/person for autonomous colonies. — [NASA USC talk on Bioregenerative Life Support, Apr 2022 (NTRS)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220006578/downloads/USC%20Talk%20on%20Bioregenerative%20Life%20Support%20April%202022.pdf) (these figures were surfaced through a search snippet)
- The BIO-Plex Biomass Production Chamber 1 design had 82 m² of crop area at a volume-to-area ratio of 2.3 m³/m² — [SAE 1999-01-2188](https://saemobilus.sae.org/content/1999-01-2188). A 2001 revision cut this to 79 m² — [SAE 2001-01-2319](https://saemobilus.sae.org/content/2001-01-2319)

**Low-pressure (hypobaric) greenhouse option**
- Mars greenhouses may run at only 0.1–0.2 atm internal pressure. Lower pressure allows lighter structures but makes heat and mass transfer harder. Lettuce was grown at 25 kPa. — [Bucklin et al. 2004, FAO AGRIS](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65df75dd4c5aef494fe2c2dd). Arabidopsis at 10 kPa versus 101 kPa showed more than 200 differentially expressed genes, many in drought pathways (water movement is the central problem) — [Paul/Ferl, PubMed 14701916](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14701916). In lettuce grown at 25 kPa, net photosynthesis was not affected by total pressure — [He et al., Photosynthetica 2013](https://ps.ueb.cas.cz/artkey/phs-201303-0017_hypobaria-and-hypoxia-affects-phytochemical-production-gas-exchange-and-growth-of-lettuce.php)

### Inferences
- For a believable "first Mars greenhouse" scene, an EDEN ISS-style container (5.7 x 2.15 x 2.55 m interior, 1.0 m aisle, 575 mm shelves on both sides, sealed door to a service room, cold-porch airlock) is the best-documented template. It could be buried under regolith or sit inside an Ice Home-style torus. A larger "outpost" greenhouse could chain several such modules, or use UA-style 2.06 x 5.5 m inflatable cylinders off a hallway.
- Planning numbers for a crew of 4: about 10–25 m²/person for partial diet supplementation, which gives roughly 40–100 m² of canopy, and ~50 m²/person for near-full food closure. Lunar Palace's 120 m² across three layers fed 4 people with 73% food closure (dry weight) — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.01.12.426282v1.full-text). A sim showing "one EDEN-sized module" is therefore a salad supplement, not a full diet.
- Pressure: nearly all built analogues run at 101 kPa. A hypobaric Mars greenhouse at 10–25 kPa is a legitimate design variant. It would change robot cooling (less convection) and the risk of leaks and condensation.

### Gaps
- No authoritative published dimensions were found for the current (post-2015) MDRS GreenHab, Mars X-House, or MARSHA's individual floor heights.
- No SpaceX greenhouse or habitat design with dimensions was found; the only public material is renderings. Mars Society design-contest entries were not researched in depth.
- The exact EDEN LUNA greenhouse dimensions in Cologne were not found. The EVE paper cites a 1.35 x 0.58 m shelf tray, which differs from the 0.6 x 0.4 m EDEN ISS Eurobox trays, so the interior may have been reconfigured.
- Whether EDEN LUNA and EVE were actually operating in Cologne by October 2026 could not be confirmed. The plans said "2025" and then "beginning of 2026".
- The BIO-Plex cancellation date and the UA LGH lamp type, wattage and yields were not found in fetched sources.

## Internal growing systems: racks, hydroponics, lighting, air, nutrients, water, sensors

### Takeaway
Space greenhouses use stacked shelves. Hydroponic or aeroponic trays hold crops in rock-wool plugs under water-cooled LED panels. The panels mix red (~630 nm), blue (~450 nm), far-red and white channels and run at 300–600 µmol/m²/s for ~17 h/day. Ducted conditioned air feeds each level. A nutrient system with two tanks doses pH and EC. Transpired water is recovered, and fixed top and side cameras photograph every tray daily. Measured whole-facility power at EDEN ISS was about 0.8 kW per m² of cultivation area.

### Cited Findings

**Racks and trays (EDEN ISS FEG)**
- 42 trays of 600 x 400 mm (standard Euroboxes, food-grade, dark grey to block stray light). Short crops use 7.5 cm deep trays with 2 misters; tall crops use 12 cm deep trays with 4 misters. Root zone height is 70 or 115 mm. Shoot zone height is 315, 865 or 1990 mm for 4-, 2- or 1-level racks. Lids have crop-specific hole patterns (tomato, lettuce, rucola). Quick-connect tubing lets a tray be removed without shutting down a rack. Plants sit in small rock-wool plugs. — [Zabel et al. 2017](https://elib.dlr.de/114876/1/Zabel%20et%20al.%20FEG%20Design%20ICES%202017_v3.pdf)
- Irrigation is combined aeroponics and NFT: 0.5 mm misters spray the roots, and once roots rest on the tray bottom the runoff flows past them as a nutrient film. — [Zabel et al. 2017](https://elib.dlr.de/114876/1/Zabel%20et%20al.%20FEG%20Design%20ICES%202017_v3.pdf)
- In 2018, 4 of the 42 trays were a nursery used for germination and seedlings for 10–30 days. Each tray's effective cultivation area is ~0.328 m², larger than the 0.24 m² tray because canopies spread. — [Zabel et al. 2019](https://elib.dlr.de/128530/1/Zabel_EDEN%20ISS%20crew%20time_v2.pdf)
- Rack R3 is assigned to cucumbers. Its lamps have two heights: low for early growth and high later. — [Zabel et al. 2017](https://elib.dlr.de/114876/1/Zabel%20et%20al.%20FEG%20Design%20ICES%202017_v3.pdf)

**Lighting**
- 42 water-cooled LED lamps, two per level, from Heliospectra (a modified LX-601). Channels are red 630 nm, blue 450 nm, far-red 735 nm and white 5700 K, each dimmable 0–100% in 0.1% steps with per-lamp schedules. Up to 300 µmol/m²/s for small plants (L2–L4) and 600 µmol/m²/s for tall plants (L1, R1–R4). A thermal cut-off turns a lamp off above 50 °C. Water cooling keeps lamp heat out of the cabin air. Photoperiod is 17 h/day. — [Zabel et al. 2017](https://elib.dlr.de/114876/1/Zabel%20et%20al.%20FEG%20Design%20ICES%202017_v3.pdf)
- The ISS Veggie unit uses red, green and blue LEDs with a single fan pulling ISS cabin air through the growth volume. — [Schuerger et al. 2021, Astrobiology](https://plantpath.ifas.ufl.edu/misc/media/Schuerger_2021_Astrobiology_F_oxy.pdf). Veggie growth area is 0.13 m² and APH is 0.2 m². — [Prince et al. IAC-24](https://elib.dlr.de/210298/1/IAC24_manuscript_Autonomous_Robotic_System_Plant_Cultivation_Luna_Surface_author_version.pdf)
- Red/blue LED images were less useful than white-light flash photographs for diagnosing the Veggie disease outbreak. — [Schuerger et al. 2021](https://plantpath.ifas.ufl.edu/misc/media/Schuerger_2021_Astrobiology_F_oxy.pdf)
- Lunar Palace 1 ran 250–800 µmol/m²/s, with 24/0 h in one cabin and 12/12 h in the other. — [bioRxiv 2021](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.01.12.426282v1.full-text)

**Power**
- EDEN ISS averaged 0.8 kW per m² of cultivated area during the Antarctic mission, less than half the 2.1 kW/m² previously assumed for space greenhouses. — [HortiDaily, DLR results](https://www.hortidaily.com/article/9137444/substantial-vegetable-harvest-in-antarctica/). A full energy breakdown by facility, subsystem and individual LED lamp is in Zabel et al. 2022 — [DLR elib 186936 record](https://openpolar.no/Record/ftdlr:oai:elib.dlr.de:186936)

**Air handling**
- The AMS sits in the Service Section. It takes warm, humid, O2-rich air from the FEG, dehumidifies and filters it, and returns cool, dry, CO2-rich air through **8 horizontal tapered supply ducts**, four per side, one per level. Each duct has 8 adjustable louvres giving ~0.4 m/s onto the canopy. Nominal flow is 1400 m³/h (~44 air changes/h), ~175 m³/h per duct and 16–20 m³/h per louvre. There are also 8 tangential fans to break up stratification, plus holes at the top and bottom of each duct to sweep air along the walls against condensation. CFD showed hot spots around trays from lamp waste heat. — [Zabel et al. 2017](https://elib.dlr.de/114876/1/Zabel%20et%20al.%20FEG%20Design%20ICES%202017_v3.pdf)

**Nutrient delivery and water**
- Two separate solution loops, each with its own feed and drain pipes in the sub-floor. 3-way valves choose which solution goes to each rack, and one high-pressure pump serves all trays in a rack. Both loops are at pH 5.8; EC is 2.5 mS/cm for leafy greens and herbs and 3.5 mS/cm for tomato and cucumber. — [Zabel et al. 2017](https://elib.dlr.de/114876/1/Zabel%20et%20al.%20FEG%20Design%20ICES%202017_v3.pdf)
- The NDS doses from concentrated stock-solution bottles (114 bottles in 9 months) and uses diluted acid and base bottles for pH. A 250 L fresh-water tank and a 250 L wastewater tank were filled and emptied by canister. Plant transpiration water is recovered and returned to the fresh-water tank. Wastewater comes only from cleaning. — [Zabel et al. 2019](https://elib.dlr.de/128530/1/Zabel_EDEN%20ISS%20crew%20time_v2.pdf)
- In Lunar Palace, plant transpiration was 724 L/day (6.13 L/m²/day). Urine went through low-pressure distillation after bio-pretreatment, with 99.7% N recovery. Grey water went through a membrane bio-activated-carbon reactor and UV. Solid waste was fermented with straw and insect residue (67% degradation). O2 and water were 100% recycled. Yellow mealworms, fed straw and leaves, supplied protein. — [bioRxiv 2021](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.01.12.426282v1.full-text)
- In the UA LGH, water is oxygenated and dosed with nutrient salts, flows continuously over the roots and returns to storage. Crew-exhaled CO2 is fed in. — [NASA 2017](https://www.nasa.gov/science-research/lunar-martian-greenhouses-designed-to-mimic-those-on-earth). Its "Cable Culture" is NFT with growing envelopes slung on cables along the cylinder axis — [UA CEAC](https://ceac.arizona.edu/about/facilities)

**Sensors and cameras (EDEN ISS)**
- **21 fixed top-view cameras** (each covering 2 trays, or 1 tray in the nursery) and **8 fixed side-view cameras**. Images are taken once a day or on demand and sent to the Bremen mission operations centre. Image-analysis software pre-screens them for human review, and the cameras can be controlled remotely. — [Zabel et al. 2017](https://elib.dlr.de/114876/1/Zabel%20et%20al.%20FEG%20Design%20ICES%202017_v3.pdf)
- Two Argus Titan Omni aspirated boxes measure temperature, RH, CO2 and PAR. Four E+E EE071 T/RH sensors are spread around the room. The NDS has 8 EC/pH sensors on its two tanks. — [Zabel et al. 2017](https://elib.dlr.de/114876/1/Zabel%20et%20al.%20FEG%20Design%20ICES%202017_v3.pdf); [Zabel et al. 2019](https://elib.dlr.de/128530/1/Zabel_EDEN%20ISS%20crew%20time_v2.pdf)

**Regolith-based growing**
- Wageningen grew 10 crops in NASA Mars and Moon regolith simulants (JSC-1A) with added nutrients and bacteria. Nine produced edible parts; spinach failed. Mars simulant did better than lunar. — [WUR, Wamelink et al.](https://research.wur.nl/en/publications/can-plants-grow-on-mars-and-the-moon-a-growth-experiment-on-mars-/); [Sci.News](https://www.sci.news/space/crops-simulated-martian-lunar-dirt-07697.html)
- Problems listed for Martian soil include perchlorates, lack of nutrients, coarse particles, no microbiome and cold (student poster, a weaker source). — [UND NASA Space Grant poster 2022](https://ndspacegrant.und.edu/_files/docs/meetings/2022/uncovering-the-effects-of-off-planet-microgravity-on-microgreens-through-enriched-martian-regolith.pdf)

**Yields (EDEN ISS 2018, for crop-growth tuning)**
- 2018 season: 67 kg cucumber, 46 kg tomato, 19 kg kohlrabi, 8 kg radish, 15 kg herbs, 56 kg lettuce, 51 kg leafy greens and 1.5 kg sweet pepper. — [Zabel et al. 2019](https://elib.dlr.de/128530/1/Zabel_EDEN%20ISS%20crew%20time_v2.pdf). That is more than 268 kg in total, or 27.4 kg/m²/yr (0.075 kg/m²/day). — [Frontiers 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpls.2020.00656/full)
- Yield per tray per cycle: lettuce 0.51–0.95 kg (37.7 d cycle); cucumber 16.69 kg/tray (50.88 kg/m², 161 d); dwarf tomato 4.3–4.9 kg/tray (286 d); radish 0.51 kg (23.3 d); Mizuna 7.58 kg; Swiss chard 3.04 kg. — [Zabel et al. 2019, Tables 4–5](https://elib.dlr.de/128530/1/Zabel_EDEN%20ISS%20crew%20time_v2.pdf)

### Inferences
- Visual look for rendering: white laminate walls, grey trays with hole-pattern lids, aluminium-profile racks, stacked thin LED panels over each level, metal floor grating and stainless sub-floor. The light is magenta or pink when red and blue dominate and near-white when the 5700 K channel is on. The 2017 test photographs show blue-only, red-only, red+blue and white modes. Simulated cameras should switch to white light for inspection, because red/blue images were worse for diagnosis.
- Sim parameters per tray: 0.6 x 0.4 m footprint, ~0.33 m² effective canopy, independent lamp channels, one of two nutrient loops, and air supplied from a side louvre. These map directly to per-tray state variables (PPFD, spectrum, EC, pH, root-zone wetness, local air speed).

### Gaps
- No exact UA LGH lamp specification or PPFD was found. CEAC and NASA pages mention only "water-cooled lamps" or LEDs and fibre-optic sunlight.
- No Lunar Palace LED spectrum, Mars Dune Alpha crop-tower dimensions, or Veggie/APH camera specifications were found in the fetched material.

## Robotics and automation for space and controlled-environment farming

### Takeaway
No robot has tended crops in an operational space greenhouse. The most concrete space system is DLR's EVE (built 2024–2026 for EDEN LUNA). It is a 7-DOF lightweight arm on a ceiling-mounted, L-shaped Beckhoff linear rail, with a soft three-finger CLASH hand and a wrist RGB-D camera, run under shared autonomy. In lab tests it harvested tomatoes in 23.5 s per fruit with 58% success. Terrestrial greenhouse robots show the motion choices that would transfer: pipe-rail carts with arms (SWEEPER, Kompano), mobile bases that move whole grow modules (Iron Ox Grover), and multi-arm harvesters (Four Growers, Tortuga). Much of the US vertical-farm robotics wave went bankrupt in 2023–2025.

### Cited Findings

**DLR EVE (EDEN Versatile End-effector) for EDEN LUNA. In development and integration 2024–2026**
- Components: a linear rail system, a 7-DOF robotic arm, the CLASH hand, a camera and a Beckhoff industrial PC, all mounted on the ceiling of the greenhouse. The rail is a Beckhoff unit, **L-shaped so the arm can park in a stowage area**. The arm comes from DLR's lightweight manipulator line and suits the very constrained space. CLASH (Compliant Low-cost Antagonistic Servo Hand) has three fingers, a palm and sensors for grasping soft fruit; the wrist rotates to approach fruit from the top, bottom, front or sides. An Intel RealSense RGB-D camera on the hand feeds perception. — [Prince et al., IAC-24](https://elib.dlr.de/210298/1/IAC24_manuscript_Autonomous_Robotic_System_Plant_Cultivation_Luna_Surface_author_version.pdf)
- Power ~700 W and mass ~170 kg installed. The rails are Beckhoff's eXtended Transport System, with a "mover" carrying the arm. The arm is based on DLR's "This Is Not an Arm" (TINA). The fingertips have force feedback. Tasks are push, pull and pick-up, and operators could work from Earth or the Lunar Gateway. — [Universe Today](https://www.universetoday.com/articles/can-a-greenhouse-with-a-robotic-arm-feed-the-next-lunar-astronauts); paper: Prince et al., "EDEN Versatile End-effector (EVE)", IEEE Aerospace Conf. 2024 — [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10521422/)
- Software: shared autonomy, where an operator command triggers autonomous execution. Hierarchical state machines compose skills such as *drive mover, move arm, identify object, grasp object*, and link to a motion planner, world model and controller. A GUI shows telemetry. EVE is meant to automate the whole cycle "from germination to harvesting". — [Prince et al., IAC-24](https://elib.dlr.de/210298/1/IAC24_manuscript_Autonomous_Robotic_System_Plant_Cultivation_Luna_Surface_author_version.pdf)
- Lab harvesting test: a KUKA LWR arm (mounted upside down relative to the final design) and CLASH hand picked artificial tomatoes from three plants of six fruit each, on a shelf tray of **1.35 x 0.58 m, the same as the final EDEN LUNA tray**. There were 36 trials at wrist angles of 0/30/45/60/90°. Mean cycle time was 23.46 s (identify, grasp, move to basket), with grasp time 12.86 s. Success 58.3%, drop 36.1%, miss 5.6%. The best results were at 0° (75%, 20.8 s) and 90° (78%, 28.5 s). Pipeline: RGB-D capture, then bounding box and segmentation against leaves, then depth, then camera-frame pose, then robot frame through hand-eye calibration. Problems: leaves occluding fruit, no reaction surface (fruit swings), and a perception model reused from bin-picking. — [Prince et al., IAC-24](https://elib.dlr.de/210298/1/IAC24_manuscript_Autonomous_Robotic_System_Plant_Cultivation_Luna_Surface_author_version.pdf)
- Robot time budget: 18 tomatoes x 23 s = 6.9 min, plus a **5 m rail traverse at 5 cm/s (100 s)** and 30 s of arm initialisation, gives 9.07 min for a 0.783 m² tray. — [Prince et al., IAC-24](https://elib.dlr.de/210298/1/IAC24_manuscript_Autonomous_Robotic_System_Plant_Cultivation_Luna_Surface_author_version.pdf)
- Survey of agricultural harvesters in the same paper: cycle times 3–94 s, mean 19.3 s; success 29.5–90%, mean 64.4%; damage averaging 16.2%. Examples: sweet pepper (Bac) 94 s/29.5%; cucumber (van Henten) 45 s/80%; strawberry (Octinion) 3 s/70%. — [Prince et al., IAC-24, Table 2](https://elib.dlr.de/210298/1/IAC24_manuscript_Autonomous_Robotic_System_Plant_Cultivation_Luna_Surface_author_version.pdf)
- Earlier DLR work (2021) described arms on roof-suspended rails that photograph plants and prune dead leaves. — [CNBC 2021](https://www.cnbc.com/2021/06/20/space-agencies-are-learning-how-to-farm-on-mars-and-the-moon.html)

**NASA KSC, Lockheed Martin and university partners: plant-tending robot (2021)**
- A 6-DOF arm, a vision system for 3D plant reconstruction, a pneumatic multi-tool changer and custom end effectors (an enclosure-latch tool to open growth chambers and a plant-shear tool), plus remote teleoperation. Aimed at autonomous plant growth systems for deep space. — [Hament, Oh et al., ASME IMECE2021-69733, UNLV repository](https://oasis.library.unlv.edu/me_fac_articles/956/)

**NASA X-Hab, CU Boulder (2013, historic)**
- Remote plant production demo at KSC Swamp Works: a robotic arm, a soil-moisture probe that adds water and nutrients, plants rotated under LEDs, and webcams. Leaf-cutting and fruit-harvest software was still in development. — [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/science-research/university-students-demonstrate-robotic-gardening-system)

**NASA Ohalo III (KSC). In development since 2019**
- A supplemental crop production system that draws on Veggie and APH. A goal is minimising crew intervention, and NASA KSC names automation, robotics and machine learning as a key challenge. — [Popular Science](https://www.popsci.com/science/nasa-plans-space-farms/); [Ohalo III project page (PRR Feb 2020)](https://salad-project.notion.site/Ohalo-III-47448073d8b542c8a7312abf0073125d)

**Mars Ice Home robotic roles (concept)**
- Pre-deployment by "basic robotic systems". Power and data cables connected robotically. External robotic visual inspection. Robotic transporters move the package from the landing zone. Teleoperated vehicles carry palletised equipment racks through a large cargo airlock; an MSL-sized vehicle needs an airlock 3 x 2.7 x 2.2 m rated for 899 kg. — [Ice Home ConOps](http://bigidea.nianet.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/IceDome-ConOps-2017-12-21v-reduced.pdf)

**Terrestrial greenhouse and vertical-farm robots that would transfer**
- **SWEEPER (EU H2020, Wageningen UR and partners; project ended ~2018).** Final commercial-greenhouse test, 262 fruits over 4 weeks: mean cycle 24 s, about half of it logistics (7.8 s fruit discharge, 4.7 s platform moves), projected 15 s with a faster arm. Success was 61% under best-fit crop conditions and 18% under current ones. — [Arad et al. 2020, J. Field Robotics (WUR record)](https://research.wur.nl/en/publications/development-of-a-sweet-pepper-harvesting-robot/). Platform: FANUC LR Mate 200iD (717 mm reach, 7 kg payload) with a custom gripper and catcher, running on greenhouse pipe rails with 3D vision — [Vision Systems Design](https://www.vision-systems.com/non-factory/scientific-industrial-research/article/16736124/autonomous-mobile-robot-aided-by-3d-vision-harvests-sweet-peppers). The predecessor test (CROPS) gave 6% (Fin Ray) and 2% (Lip) success, rising to 26%/33% with occluding leaves removed; cycle time 94 s against an economic target of 6 s — [Bac et al., arXiv 1706.06203](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.06203)
- **Priva Kompano (tomato de-leafing, commercial).** Runs on the existing heating-pipe rails between high-wire tomato rows, with a vision system and a telescopic cutter. It moves plant to plant, uses barcodes at path ends and needs no GNSS, LiDAR or camera navigation. Priva claims >85% effectiveness over 1 ha/week, running 24/7. — [Priva](https://www.priva.com/blog/horticulture/kompano-automated-leaf-cutting-robot); [Future Farming](https://futurefarming.com/tech-in-focus/field-robots/kompano-is-fully-automated-leaf-cutting-robot-for-tomato-crops). Each robot covers 0.75–1 ha depending on stem density; a 50-unit series was built by MTA — [GreenTech](https://www.greentech.nl/news/robotics/priva-introduced-kompano-the-worlds-first-fully-automated-leaf-cutting-robot-for-tomato-crops)
- **Iron Ox Grover (defunct).** An autonomous mobile robot that lifts hydroponic grow modules of about 6 x 6 ft and ~1,000 lb (capacity >1,000 lb). Differential drive, multiple LiDARs, forward- and upward-facing cameras. It carries modules to a scanning booth, then to watering, nutrient or harvest stations. — [AgroSpectrum](https://agrospectrumindia.com/news/64/26416/iron-ox-launches-autonomous-mobile-robot-grover.html); [New Atlas](https://newatlas.com/robotics/grover-greenhouse-robot-iron-ox-farms/?amp=true). Iron Ox has ceased operations, and part of its technology went to Inevitable Tech — [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/agtech-foodtech/vertical-farming-venture-capital-plenty-appharvest/)
- **Four Growers GR-100 (tomato harvesting).** Four stereo cameras, a robotic arm and remote monitoring; it picks, quality-checks and stores fruit. Source is a third-party profile and should be treated cautiously. — [Extruct profile](https://www.extruct.ai/hub/fourgrowers-com/)
- **Tortuga AgTech, acquired by Oishii in 2025.** A fleet of 150 strawberry-picking robots. Oishii already runs ~50 of its own robots in its main vertical farm and works with Yaskawa; it projects up to 50% lower harvest cost (company claim). — [AgFunderNews](https://agfundernews.com/oishii-acquires-tortuga-agtechs-ip-assets-and-engineering-team-to-supercharge-its-vertical-farms); [Future Farming](https://futurefarming.com/tech-in-focus/field-robots/oishii-acquires-tortuga-agtech-to-scale-robotic-strawberry-harvesting)
- **Plenty** filed Chapter 11 in March 2025 after raising nearly $1B. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/24/vertical-farming-company-plenty-files-for-bankruptcy-after-raising-nearly-1b); it closed its South San Francisco facility — [AgFunderNews](https://agfundernews.com/plenty-confirms-closure-of-south-san-francisco-vertical-farming-facility)
- **AppHarvest**, which bought Root AI (a harvesting-robot maker) in 2021, filed Chapter 11 on 23 Jul 2023, and its greenhouses were sold off. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AppHarvest); [Food Dive](https://www.fooddive.com/news/appharvest-bankruptcy-indoor-farming-martha-stewart-jd-vance/689040/)
- Context: a 2024 scoping review notes that robot harvesting is generally much slower than human picking and suggests human-robot collaboration first. — [Computers and Electronics in Agriculture, scoping review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0168169924001418). One blog counts 14 CEA bankruptcies in 2025 (unverified aggregator) — [foodlore](https://foodlore.blog/why-vertical-farms-go-bankrupt/)

### Inferences
- Three robot archetypes fit a Mars greenhouse sim:
  - (a) A **ceiling or overhead rail-mounted 6–7 DOF arm** (EVE). It suits narrow fixed-shelf rooms like the 1.0 m FEG aisle, because a floor robot competes with the crew for space.
  - (b) A **rail or aisle cart with an arm and RGB-D camera** (SWEEPER, Kompano). It suits long rows of tall vine crops.
  - (c) A **mobile lifter that moves whole trays or modules** to fixed stations (Grover). It suits larger floor-plan greenhouses or Ice Home floors where the robot brings plants to an imaging or harvest station.
- Realistic performance targets for sim robots: ~20–25 s per fruit, ~60% first-attempt success, and a significant drop or damage rate (EVE dropped 36%). Failures come from leaf occlusion and swinging fruit. Rail speeds around 5 cm/s are realistic for a ceiling mover in a cluttered space.
- Tools worth modelling: a soft three-finger gripper, plant shears, a latch tool for chamber doors, a tool changer, and a downward or side RGB-D camera. Fixed inspection cameras complement the robot (EDEN ISS: 21 top and 8 side cameras).

### Gaps
- No EVE operational results from EDEN LUNA in Cologne (2025–2026) were found. Only lab results from 2024 are published in the fetched material.
- No evidence was found of robotic crop tending in Lunar Palace 1; operations appear to be crew-manual.
- Fifth Season's status and robots were not confirmed. Wageningen greenhouse robotics after SWEEPER, Harvey (QUT) specifications and Ridder/Priva harvesting robots were not researched in detail.

## Daily and seasonal operations and failure responses (simulation scenarios)

### Takeaway
Measured EDEN ISS practice gives a ready-made task catalogue in four clusters: Crop Cultivation, Maintenance, Repair and Science, with minutes per tray. Maintenance, mostly nutrient-system work, took more crew time than plant tending. Documented failures span cooling-loop leaks, fan failures, nutrient-tank biofilm and nutrient deficiencies, fungal outbreaks from wet leaves and poor airflow (ISS Veggie), CO2 excursions, and power outages (Lunar Palace).

### Cited Findings

**Task taxonomy (EDEN ISS, Zabel et al. 2019)**
- **Crop Cultivation:** sowing, thinning, pruning, harvesting, pollination, moving plants (nursery to tray), plant health monitoring, food quality and safety, separating edible from inedible biomass.
- **Maintenance:** filter exchange and cleaning, cleaning the cultivation area, telemetry checks, nutrient solution preparation and adjustment, sensor calibration, filling, emptying and cleaning tanks, consumables supply, waste management.
- **Repair:** swapping broken parts for spares, repairing, manufacturing spares, contingency actions to protect equipment, plants and crew, spill cleaning, system shutdown.
- **Science:** experiments and sampling. — [Zabel et al. 2019](https://elib.dlr.de/128530/1/Zabel_EDEN%20ISS%20crew%20time_v2.pdf)

**Measured crew time per task (per tray)**
- Lettuce (6 plants/tray): sowing ~1:35 min, transfer 1:15, harvest 1:18, clearing inedible biomass 2:54.
- Radish (36/tray): sowing ~5 min; thinning from 2 seeds to 1 per block ~1:33.
- Cucumber (2/tray): pruning 11–18 min (mean 14:18), harvest ~1:14 each time.
- Dwarf tomato (4/tray): pruning ~16 min, harvest 4:26–5:38.
- Herbs and leafy greens: repeated "spread harvesting" (cut mature leaves, leave young ones). Parsley took 9–34 min per harvest and was harvested 22 times per cycle.
- Arugula, chives and parsley were sown on fibre mats (>100 plants/tray), and sowing took ~10 min. — [Zabel et al. 2019, Table 2](https://elib.dlr.de/128530/1/Zabel_EDEN%20ISS%20crew%20time_v2.pdf)
- Cycle lengths: lettuce ~37.7 d, radish 23.3 d, arugula 28.1 d, red mustard 79.8 d, basil 121 d, cucumber 161 d, dwarf tomato 286 d. Cucumber had the lowest crew time per kg (18 min/kg); herbs, radish and dwarf tomato were the least efficient (~1:12–1:50 h/kg). — [Zabel et al. 2019, Tables 4–5](https://elib.dlr.de/128530/1/Zabel_EDEN%20ISS%20crew%20time_v2.pdf)

**Maintenance cadence (9-month season)**
- Daily system and plant check, in person or remote: ~15 min/day.
- Lamp intensity adjustments as tomato, pepper, cucumber and basil grew: minutes every few months.
- AMS: one coarse filter swap (~10 min) and two CO2 bottle swaps (~1.5 h each, including transport).
- Two EC/pH calibrations of all 8 sensors (~1.5 h each).
- 114 stock-solution bottles (8–11 min each) plus micronutrient mixes (~17.5 min each).
- Acid and base bottles ~3 min each.
- **Tank drain, clean and refill 7 times, ~5 h per tank, needed because of biofilm and calcium/potassium deficiency symptoms.**
- 14 fresh-water fills and 8 wastewater removals (~1.5 h each).
- ~10 h of cleaning.
- Totals: maintenance 174 h/season = 18.3 h/month ≈ 28.9 min per tray per month (1:28 h per m² per month). Maintenance exceeded plant-tending time. — [Zabel et al. 2019](https://elib.dlr.de/128530/1/Zabel_EDEN%20ISS%20crew%20time_v2.pdf)
- Science sampling: monthly microbial swabs at 20 locations x 4 swabs (~1 h); monthly plant microbial samples, 10 crops x 4 (~1:10 h); nitrate measurements (~1:05 h per harvest). — [Zabel et al. 2019](https://elib.dlr.de/128530/1/Zabel_EDEN%20ISS%20crew%20time_v2.pdf)
- Typical workday: telemetry and office work and supply preparation in the morning; one afternoon greenhouse visit (because getting there took 20–40 min each way, including dressing for the polar weather) for system and plant checks, harvest, sowing and nursery transfer; then harvest processing, food-safety sampling, freeze-drying and a 45–120 min contact with mission control. — [Zabel et al. 2019, Tables 6–7](https://elib.dlr.de/128530/1/Zabel_EDEN%20ISS%20crew%20time_v2.pdf)
- Crew-time rates compared across missions (min/day/m²): Veggie 21.5, APH ~14, HI-SEAS II 31.2, MDRS 9, ILMAH 24, EDEN ISS 11.5 (143 min/day for 12.5 m²), BIOS-3 16 (600 min/day for 37.5 m²). EVE's robot harvest estimate is 11.58 min/m². NASA's figure for crew time is ~$130,000/h. — [Prince et al., IAC-24](https://elib.dlr.de/210298/1/IAC24_manuscript_Autonomous_Robotic_System_Plant_Cultivation_Luna_Surface_author_version.pdf)
- Season shape (EDEN ISS 2018): first sowing 7 Feb, first harvest of lettuce, radish and chard 20 Mar, first cucumber 29 Mar, first tomato 16 May, last harvest 20 Nov. A single operator ran it, with help from others at critical stages such as harvest. — [Zabel et al. 2019](https://elib.dlr.de/128530/1/Zabel_EDEN%20ISS%20crew%20time_v2.pdf); [Zabel et al. 2017](https://elib.dlr.de/114876/1/Zabel%20et%20al.%20FEG%20Design%20ICES%202017_v3.pdf). By June 2018: 120+ harvests, 120 lettuce heads, 315 cucumbers, 179 radishes and 1,502 tomatoes — [EDEN ISS blog](https://eden-iss.net/index.php/2018/06/04/analogue-mission-antarctica-several-months-in-and-running/)
- Lunar Palace 365: 8 h work and 8 h sleep per day. Two crews of 4 rotated at days 60 and 260 with ~7 h of overlap. Duties included solid waste, urine, shredding straw, threshing wheat, food processing, crop growing, health monitoring and inventory. — [bioRxiv 2021](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.01.12.426282v1.full-text); [BUAA / China Daily](https://news.buaa.edu.cn/info/1006/31310.htm)

**Documented failures and contingencies**
- EDEN ISS 2018: a leak in a thermal-system cooling loop, a failed fan unit, longer heater activation in extreme cold, and minor external damage from a storm with >70 kn winds and 84 kn gusts; the lowest temperature was −38.3 °C. All were repaired. — [EDEN ISS blog, Jun 2018](https://eden-iss.net/index.php/2018/06/04/analogue-mission-antarctica-several-months-in-and-running/). Repair time was not tracked despite "numerous off-nominal events" — [Zabel et al. 2019](https://elib.dlr.de/128530/1/Zabel_EDEN%20ISS%20crew%20time_v2.pdf)
- Design-level failure handling in the FEG: a floor basin catches all leaks (pump to wastewater), LEDs cut out above 50 °C, and quick-connect trays can be pulled without stopping the rack. — [Zabel et al. 2017](https://elib.dlr.de/114876/1/Zabel%20et%20al.%20FEG%20Design%20ICES%202017_v3.pdf)
- ISS Veggie zinnias, late 2015: low airflow let water collect (guttation), causing chlorosis, leaf curl and mould. The presumptive pathogen was *Fusarium oxysporum*. The crew sanitised, wiped off free water and raised the fan speed. Scott Kelly was given autonomy to manage the plants. — [Schuerger et al. 2021](https://plantpath.ifas.ufl.edu/misc/media/Schuerger_2021_Astrobiology_F_oxy.pdf); [Universe Today](https://www.universetoday.com/articles/space-zinnias-rebound-space-blight-space-station); [Space.com](https://space.com/31556-space-fungus-attacks-space-station-plants.html)
- Lunar Palace 365: CO2 spikes after each crew change. A deliberate wheat-growth inhibition test (days 200–260) caused a CO2 rise, recovered by changing photoperiods and fermentation rates. **Three simulated power outages of 2, 3 and 4 h made CO2 rise 251–312 ppm/h, restabilising 12–24 h after power returned.** Acrolein and ozone slightly exceeded NASA limits. — [bioRxiv 2021](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.01.12.426282v1.full-text)
- MDRS GreenHab was destroyed by a fire from an electrical heater on 29 Dec 2014. — [Space.com](https://www.space.com/28388-greenhouse-fire-mock-mars-mission.html)

### Inferences
- Candidate sim scenarios grounded in these sources:
  - Daily inspection sweep: image every tray and flag anomalies.
  - Sowing rock-wool plugs in nursery trays, then transplanting to cultivation trays after 10–30 days.
  - Thinning radish.
  - Weekly pruning of tomato and cucumber side shoots and dead leaves.
  - Spread harvesting of leafy greens.
  - Whole-head lettuce harvest and clearing the tray.
  - Fruit picking into a basket.
  - Moving cucumber lamps from the low to the high position.
  - Swapping a stock-solution bottle and acid/base dosing.
  - Sensor calibration.
  - Tank drain, clean and refill after biofilm.
  - Swapping filters and CO2 bottles.
  - Water canister logistics.
  - Microbial swabbing.
  - Failure injections: a cooling-loop or mister leak into the floor basin; a fan failure causing stratification, condensation and mould; a lamp over-temperature cut-out; a pump failure on one rack; nutrient deficiency symptoms; a Fusarium outbreak on wet foliage; a CO2 spike; a 2–4 h power loss; an electrical fire.
- Because maintenance exceeds plant tending, a believable robot mission mix should include valve, bottle, tank and filter manipulation, not only harvesting.

### Gaps
- No systematic public list of EDEN ISS failures for 2019–2021 was fetched; the Bamsey 2018 COSPAR lessons-learned paper and the 2019–2020 season paper were not accessed.
- No pollination procedure (for tomato or pepper) was documented in fetched sources. It is listed as a task, but its method and time were not measured.

## Outdoor-to-indoor logistics: airlocks, regolith collection, moving produce and supplies

### Takeaway
Concepts put a cold porch or airlock between outside and the greenhouse. Mars Ice Home adds a large cargo airlock sized for an MSL-class teleoperated rover (3 x 2.7 x 2.2 m) and soft-goods tunnels between modules. Suitports are the leading idea for keeping abrasive dust out. Logistics in the analogues was mostly people carrying canisters and bottles. Rover-to-greenhouse regolith delivery for soil growing appears only in informal discussion.

### Cited Findings
- EDEN ISS: a Cold Porch airlock leads into the Service Section, and a sealed door leads from there to the FEG. — [Zabel et al. 2017](https://elib.dlr.de/114876/1/Zabel%20et%20al.%20FEG%20Design%20ICES%202017_v3.pdf). Supplies travelled 400 m by foot: DI-water canisters in, wastewater canisters out, CO2 bottles and filters carried in (45 min including dressing), stock-solution bottles mixed in the station lab, and produce carried back for processing. Snow had to be shovelled from the entrance after storms. — [Zabel et al. 2019](https://elib.dlr.de/128530/1/Zabel_EDEN%20ISS%20crew%20time_v2.pdf)
- Mars Ice Home: a soft-goods passage connects to the crew airlock to transfer equipment and supplies. Large items enter by robotic vehicle through a cargo airlock, and robotic vehicles bring palletised equipment through a large external airlock that must work with dirt and dust. A second airlock links to a separately landed module. The Mars atmosphere is replaced with breathable air before the crew arrives. The cargo airlock for an MSL-class teleoperated vehicle is 3 m long x 2.7 m wide x 2.2 m high, rated for 899 kg ("roughly the size of a garage door"). Power is ~40 kW nuclear, and pressurised rovers are planned for a crew of 2 with 100 km of range. — [Ice Home ConOps](http://bigidea.nianet.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/IceDome-ConOps-2017-12-21v-reduced.pdf)
- Hatch, airlock, suitlock and suitport compared: a suitport, with the suit sealed to the bulkhead and no outer airlock door, gives dust mitigation similar to a suitlock at lower cost but more risk and less room for contingencies. — [Jones, SAE 2007-01-3210](https://saemobilus.sae.org/papers/planetary-airlock-suitlock-requirements-alternate-approaches-2007-01-3210). Martian dust is more abrasive than Earth dust, and a suitport keeps the suit outside the habitat's air path — [UND Human Spaceflight Lab](https://aero.und.edu/space/human-spaceflight-lab/projects/suitport.html)
- MARSHA puts the "garage" for external systems and exploration, with a wet lab, on the ground level, below the living levels and the hydroponic garden. — [livinspaces](https://livinspaces.net/ls-tv/ai-spacefactory-use-3d-printing-technology-to-develop-marsha-a-possible-habitat-for-mars/)
- Mars Dune Alpha has a 1,200 ft² red-sand sandbox outside the 3D-printed structure for simulated Marswalks (the airlock configuration was not detailed in fetched sources). Mission 2 activities also reportedly included a brick-making machine and a weather station. — [Space.com tour](https://www.space.com/nasa-mock-mars-dune-alpha-chapea-habitat-video-tour); [autoevolution](https://www.autoevolution.com/news/second-crew-of-mars-astronauts-nasa-locked-up-for-a-year-are-coming-out-on-october-31-277265.html)
- Soil-based growing needs regolith brought indoors and treated, because of perchlorates, no nutrients, coarse particles and no microbiome. — [UND poster](https://ndspacegrant.und.edu/_files/docs/meetings/2022/uncovering-the-effects-of-off-planet-microgravity-on-microgreens-through-enriched-martian-regolith.pdf). Lunar Palace made its own "soil-like substrate" from fermented solid waste — [bioRxiv 2021](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.01.12.426282v1.full-text). In The Martian, Watney moved Martian soil indoors by hand and inoculated it with Earth soil and waste — [LitCharts](https://www.litcharts.com/lit/the-martian/chapter-2)

### Inferences
- The sim's logistics graph could be: outside worksite (regolith pile, ISRU water, rover dock), then a cargo airlock or garage (3 x 2.7 x 2.2 m for rover-scale carts), then a dust-control zone or suitport, then a service room (tanks, dosing, sink, workbench, stock bottles, filters, CO2 bottles), then a sealed door, then the greenhouse aisle. A soft tunnel to the habitat kitchen carries produce.
- Robot logistics tasks: a rover bins regolith and passes it through the airlock to a soil-treatment station (washing out perchlorates, adding compost or substrate made from waste); canister and bottle shuttles between the service room and the greenhouse; tray shuttles between the nursery, cultivation racks and an imaging or harvest station; produce crates sent to the kitchen; inedible biomass sent to a composter or fermenter.
