# Growing crops on gearbox's barren Mars

The believable way to grow plants on Mars in gearbox is not Watney's open regolith floor. It is a sealed, LED-lit, soil-free growth module modelled on DLR's EDEN ISS container, set on the procedural Mars gearbox already draws, with every crop plant given an identity, a few numbers of physiological state and a growth clock that runs apart from physics. The science fixes the targets: outside, Mars offers 0.6 kPa of mostly CO2, a −59 °C mean, roughly 1000× Earth's DNA-damaging UV and dust storms that can hide the sun for weeks, while plants inside need about 10–50 kPa total pressure with at least 5–7 kPa of O2, 0.10–0.20 kPa of CO2, 20–26 °C, about 75% humidity and 300–800 µmol/m²/s of light. The maths of growth is cheap: NASA's Modified Energy Cascade (MEC) gives a tested O2, CO2 and water ledger, and a Van Henten or LINTUL-style per-plant core adds the temperature response MEC lacks, at a few dozen floating-point operations per plant per step. Gearbox already has the planet, USD world loading with static colliders, lights and glass, Isaac-verified arm and gantry joints and a "harvest on contact" event, and Bevy 0.19.1, the version gearbox pins, ships a physically based Mars atmosphere. What gearbox lacks entirely is the plant: its vegetation is a stateless GPU hash that depth, LiDAR and recognition cannot see, and the sim has no temperature, CO2 or humidity, no sol day-night cycle and no time scale. Those dependencies set the build order: clock and sky, then site and shell, climate and light, plant state, plant rendering, plant colliders, a plants API with harvest actions, and failure scenarios last. Robots come late because they are the part gearbox already does best.

## Mars kills plants outdoors, so the greenhouse must supply everything but some light

### Outside: 0.6 kPa, −59 °C and a sun that can vanish for weeks

The Martian surface fails every requirement a plant has except light. Mean surface pressure is **6.36 mbar (0.636 kPa)**, varying from 4.0 to 8.7 mbar with the seasons, in an atmosphere that is 95.1% CO2. The mean surface temperature is about 214 K (−59 °C), and the Viking 1 site swung between −89 and −31 °C within a sol ([NASA NSSDC](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/marsfact.html)). With no ozone layer, Earth's DNA-weighted UV dose is only about **0.1% of Mars's**, and photosystem damage on Mars runs about 345× Earth's ([Cockell et al. 2000](https://faculty.washington.edu/dcatling/papers_mine/Cockell_Catling2000.pdf)). Curiosity measured cosmic radiation of 0.67 mSv per day at the surface ([SwRI](https://www.swri.org/press-release/swri-scientists-publish-first-radiation-measurements-surface-mars)). Light is the exception. Top-of-atmosphere irradiance averages **586.2 W/m², 43% of Earth's** ([NASA NSSDC](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/marsfact.html)), and growth experiments mimic Mars daylight with 44–60% of Earth light ([Eos](https://eos.org/articles/tests-indicate-which-edible-plants-could-thrive-on-mars); [ScienceAlert](https://www.sciencealert.com/earthworms-can-reproduce-in-fertilised-mars-soil-simulant)).

That light cannot be trusted. Clear-day dust optical depth (tau) ranges from 0.1 to 1.0; a local storm reaches about 2 and a severe one about 6 ([Cockell et al. 2000](https://faculty.washington.edu/dcatling/papers_mine/Cockell_Catling2000.pdf)). Opportunity recorded **tau ≈ 10.8 on 10 June 2018**, the highest ever measured at the surface, and the images it took that month were its last ([Planetary Society](https://www.planetary.org/explore/space-topics/space-missions/mer-updates/2018/06-mer-update-opportunity-dust-storm-sleep.html); [PIA22930](https://pds-rings.seti.org/press_releases/pages/PIA22xxx/PIA22930.html)). A sim greenhouse that feeds crops on sunlight must carry LED backup or watch its crops stall through a global storm, the same failure that killed Opportunity. Gearbox does not need to invent the light model: the Appelbaum & Flood equations are the engineering standard for direct, diffuse and global insolation as a function of latitude, season and tau ([NTRS 19910005804](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19910005804)), and the R package `marsrad` implements them ([CRAN marsrad](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/marsrad/readme/README.html)). The clock is different too: a sol lasts 24.6597 h and the axis tilts 25.19° ([NASA NSSDC](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/marsfact.html)), and a year runs 669.6 sols, with seasons of 142 to 194 sols ([NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/mars/facts/)).

### Inside: plants tolerate a tenth of an atmosphere if the gases are right

Plants handle far lower pressure than people do. NASA Kennedy researchers put the floor for normal plant function at **about 10 kPa**, where lettuce lost water 6.1× faster than at 101 kPa ([NTRS 20050182975](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20050182975)). Oxygen partial pressure limits growth more than total pressure does: radish grew equally well at 98, 66, 33 and 10 kPa as long as pO2 stayed at or above 7 kPa, and fell at 2 kPa whatever the total ([CORDIS radish report](https://www.cordis.europa.eu/docs/results/272/272520/final1-radish-2012.pdf)). Lettuce at 25 kPa kept its gas exchange and growth, but ethylene building up above 1000 ppb in the closed chamber cut both ([ASHS 2011](https://ashs.confex.com/ashs/2011/webprogram/Paper7238.html)). Low pressure still leaves a mark: Arabidopsis at 10 kPa switched on hypoxia and drought genes while fully watered ([Zhou et al. 2017](https://frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2017.00528/full)).

Proposed Mars greenhouses sit at 10–20 kPa ([Bucklin et al. 2004](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65df75dd4c5aef494fe2c2dd)), but that collides with structures: one analysis found Mars-buildable materials hold only about 6 kPa ([SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/?p=40051)). This gap between what plants need and what local shells can hold is why EDEN ISS, like nearly every built analogue, simply runs at 101 kPa ([Zabel et al. 2017](https://elib.dlr.de/114876/1/Zabel%20et%20al.%20FEG%20Design%20ICES%202017_v3.pdf)). NASA's life-support baseline sets **pCO2 at 0.10–0.20 kPa** (nominal 0.13 kPa) and humidity near 75%, notes that wheat and potato prefer temperatures below crew comfort, and points out that CO2 is the one input Mars gives freely, since the outside air is 95% CO2 ([NASA BVAD 2018](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20180001338)). For the sim, pressure becomes a real axis. Below about 10 kPa crops fail through water stress and hypoxia, between 10 and 33 kPa growth takes a penalty while transpiration climbs, and a module shared with crew stays at 56–101 kPa.

Per-crop targets come straight from NASA's design tables and are the numbers a species file should hold:

| Crop | Light (mol/m²/d) | Photoperiod (h) | Days to harvest | Day/night °C | Edible dry yield (g/m²/d) | Harvest index |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lettuce | 17 | 16 | 28 | 23/23 | 6.57 | 90% |
| Radish | 26 | 16 | 25 | 23/23 | 5.50 | 50% |
| Tomato | 27 | 12 | 85 | 24/24 | 10.43 | 45% |
| Soybean | 28 | 12 | 97 | 26/22 | 4.54 | 40% |
| Wheat | 115 | 20–24 | 75–90 | 20/20 | 20.00 | 40% |
| White potato | 28 | 12 | 132 | 20/16 | 21.06 | 70% |
| Sweet potato | 28 | 12 | 85 | 26/22 | 24.7 | 60% |

Source: BVAD Tables 4-96 and 4-97 ([NASA BVAD 2018](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20180001338)).

The same document bounds the light range: NASA's crop models hold for 200–1000 µmol/m²/s and 330–1300 ppm CO2, and lettuce is held to 200–500 µmol/m²/s because more light causes leaf tipburn. Too little light keeps the canopy from closing at all; lettuce at 200 µmol/m²/s and 330 ppm absorbs at most 18% of incident light, against 75% at 300 µmol/m²/s ([NASA BVAD 2018](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20180001338)). Real systems sit inside that band: EDEN ISS lamps deliver up to 300 µmol/m²/s for small plants and 600 for tall ones over a 17 h day ([Zabel et al. 2017](https://elib.dlr.de/114876/1/Zabel%20et%20al.%20FEG%20Design%20ICES%202017_v3.pdf)), and China's Lunar Palace ran 250–800 µmol/m²/s ([Lunar Palace 365, bioRxiv 2021](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.01.12.426282.full.pdf)).

### Raw regolith is a germination poison that robots must process

Phoenix found **0.4–0.6% perchlorate** in Martian soil ([DLR elib 105915](https://elib.dlr.de/105915)). In a Gusev-crater simulant, any perchlorate dose tested (0.5 or 1 wt%) stopped germination in every treatment ([Oze et al. 2021](https://www.mdpi.com/2571-8789/5/3/37)). The famous Wageningen results are real but optimistic. Their 2014 trial found the Mars simulant JSC Mars-1A gave the highest germination and biomass of three soils for 11 of 14 species ([Wamelink et al. 2014](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0103138)). In the 2019 trial, 9 of 10 crops grew with added organic matter, only spinach failed, and radish, rye and cress set seed for a second generation ([Sci.News](https://www.sci.news/space/crops-simulated-martian-lunar-dirt-07697.html)). Heavy metals in the harvest stayed far below limits ([WUR](https://www.wur.nl/en/newsarticle/Dinner-of-Mars-and-moon-vegetables-well-received.htm)), and earthworms reproduced in manured simulant ([ScienceAlert](https://www.sciencealert.com/earthworms-can-reproduce-in-fertilised-mars-soil-simulant)). But JSC Mars-1A is a weathered Hawaiian tephra that contains carbon and traces of nitrogen ([Wamelink et al. 2014](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0103138)). The mineralogically faithful MGS-1 proved toxic to plants at Florida Tech despite attempts to make it viable ([Florida Tech](https://news.fit.edu/academics-research/researching-regolith-for-plant-growth/)), and a 2026 thesis restored germination only with plant-growth-promoting bacteria ([FIT etd/1671](https://repository.fit.edu/etd/1671)).

Amendments work. Compost raised seed production 61.9% over pure simulant ([Frontiers 2025](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2025.1676285/text)). Black soldier fly frass at 5–32% let peas match soil germination ([Astrobiology.com](https://astrobiology.com/2025/11/28/peas-on-mars-a-study-of-garden-pea-lathyrus-oleraceus-pisum-sativum-growth-in-martian-regolith-simulant-treated-with-black-soldier-fly-frass/)), rhizobia-inoculated clover grew 75% more ([The Debrief](https://thedebrief.org/new-astroagriculture-research-may-have-just-solved-how-we-grow-food-on-mars/)), and microbially purified perchlorate water brought wheat and lettuce to 95% germination ([BUAA](https://research.buaa.edu.cn/zh/publications/microbial-purification-of-perchlorate-in-a-simulated-martian-wate/)). Still, hydroponics has the higher technology readiness ([FIT etd/1663](https://repository.fit.edu/etd/1663)), and no head-to-head comparison with regolith exists for any space crop ([Planetary Society](https://www.planetary.org/sci-tech/growing-veggies-moon-mars)). The sim should therefore default to hydroponic or aeroponic racks. Regolith beds belong to a later scenario where germination is zero while perchlorate is present and the remediation chain becomes robot work: dig, wash or bioreduce, amend with 5–30% compost or frass, and inoculate legumes.

### Growing area decides whether the story is salad or survival

Ray Wheeler's sizing rule is that 20–25 m² of crops supply one person's oxygen and 40–50 m² their calories ([Wheeler, SIMOC](https://simoc.space/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/When-Can-Plants-Begin-to-Contribute-to-Atmospheric-Management-WHEELER.pdf)), while early missions will have under 5 m² ([Johnson et al. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/astronomy-and-space-sciences/articles/10.3389/fspas.2021.734343/text)). BVAD's diets need 1.35 m² per crew member for salad alone, 19.5 m² with carbohydrate crops and 65.29 m² for its all-crop diet ([NASA BVAD 2018](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20180001338)). The real benchmarks line up. EDEN ISS grew **268 kg of food on 12.5 m² in about 9.5 months** in Antarctica, fresh produce for an overwintering crew of 10 that still relied on stored food ([DLR](https://www.dlr.de/en/latest/news/2018/3/20180913_greenhouse-braves-the-antarctic-dlr-researcher-paul-zabel-experiences-record-harvest-and-instructive-challenges_29756)) at 0.8 kW per m², against the 2.1 kW/m² previously assumed ([IO+](https://ioplus.nl/archive/en/eden-iss-substantial-vegetable-harvest-in-antarctica/)). Lunar Palace 1 used **120 m² of planting area to give 4 people 100% of their O2 and water and 73% of their food** (dry mass) over 370 days ([bioRxiv 2021](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.01.12.426282.full.pdf)). Biosphere 2's 2000 m² of soil farming gave about 80% of the food but the crew lost 10–20% of body weight ([ecotechnics.edu](https://ecotechnics.edu/?p=189)). Water dominates the plumbing. Wheat transpires 11.79 kg/m²/d, potato 4.00 and lettuce 2.10, and growing more than 25% of the food locally lets transpiration regenerate all the water, while 50% regenerates all the air ([NASA BVAD 2018](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20180001338)). One EDEN-sized module in gearbox is a salad bar. A "crew of four survives on Mars" scenario needs on the order of 160–200 m² of high-light canopy.

### The Martian gets the biology right and misses the chemistry

Watney covers the 92 m² Hab floor with 10 cm of soil ([LitCharts ch. 2](https://www.litcharts.com/lit/the-martian/chapter-2)), extends it with bunks and pop-tents to 126 m² and estimates about 850 kcal/day ([LitCharts ch. 3](https://www.litcharts.com/lit/the-martian/chapter-3); another chapter summary gives 62 m² and 288 kcal/day, so the figures need checking against the novel), and makes water by decomposing hydrazine over iridium and burning the hydrogen ([LSU](https://faculty.lsu.edu/maverick/martian.php)). The outline holds up: Weir's point that the soil lacks "nutrients and biological materials" is correct, but he did not know about perchlorates and thinks they can be rinsed out, which costs water that must be boiled off and recaptured ([Modern Farmer](https://modernfarmer.com/2015/10/can-you-grow-plants-on-mars/)). My arithmetic on BVAD's ideal-chamber potato yield of 21.06 g edible dry mass per m² per day ([NASA BVAD 2018](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20180001338)) gives about 2.65 kg of dry tubers a day from 126 m², roughly 9,500 kcal at an assumed 3.6 kcal per dry gram. Watney's 850 kcal is thus very conservative for a lab chamber but plausible for dim Hab light, a 10 cm root zone and raw regolith. His reported first harvest at 48 sols ([Warwick](https://warwick.ac.uk/newsandevents/knowledgecentre/science/physics-astrophysics/growing_potatoes_on_mars), from a search snippet) is far ahead of BVAD's 105–132-day potato cycle. The real counterpart is the International Potato Center's Mars-motivated work, which grew salt-tolerant potatoes in Peru's Pampas de La Joya desert soil once fertilised soil was added ([CIP](https://cipotato.org/blog/indicators-show-potatoes-can-grow-mars/)). A faithful Watney scenario in gearbox would have his seed potatoes refuse to sprout until the regolith is washed, which makes a better robot task than anything in the novel.

## Two small models and a separate clock are enough to grow the plants

### MEC keeps the life-support ledger but gets growth wrong

NASA's Energy Cascade started as a wheat model (Volk et al. 1995), was extended to nine crops, and became the Modified Energy Cascade in Cavazzoni's 2001 and 2004 work ([Boscheri et al. 2012](https://simoc.space/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/Boscheri-et-al-2012.pdf)). Its core is a handful of lines. Canopy absorption rises as `A = A_MAX·(t/t_A)^n` until canopy closure at `t_A`, then stays at `A_MAX = 0.93`. Daily carbon gain is `DCG = 0.0036 · H · CUE24 · A · CQY · PPF` in mol C/m²/d, where H is photoperiod in hours and PPF is in µmol/m²/s. Crop growth is `CGR = 12.011 · DCG / BCF` in g/m²/d, oxygen output is `OPF · DCG`, and edible biomass is `XFRT · ∫CGR dt` from `t_E` to maturity `t_M` ([NASA BVAD 2018](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20180001338); [Amitrano et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7308940/)). The per-crop constants are published:

| Crop | n | CUE_max | BCF | OPF (mol O2/mol C) | XFRT | t_E / t_Q / t_M (days after emergence) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lettuce | 2.5 | 0.625 | 0.40 | 1.08 | 0.95 | 1 / – / 30 |
| Wheat | 1.0 | 0.64 | 0.44 | 1.07 | 1.00 | 34 / 33 / 62 |
| White potato | 2.0 | 0.625 | 0.41 | 1.02 | 1.00 | 45 / 75 / 138 |
| Soybean | 1.5 | 0.65 | 0.46 | 1.16 | 0.95 | 46 / 48 / 86 |
| Tomato | 2.5 | 0.65 | 0.42 | 1.09 | 0.70 | 41 / 56 / 80 |

Source: BVAD Tables 4-103 to 4-119 ([NASA BVAD 2018](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20180001338)). The polynomial coefficients for `t_A` and `CQY_MAX` as functions of PPF and CO2 sit in BVAD Tables 4-106 to 4-128 and were not extracted for this report; they must be copied from the BVAD PDF before coding.

Boscheri's hourly variant multiplies carbon gain by a light switch `I` (1 lit, 0 dark), so a lamp outage registers within the hour. Its transpiration term, `HTR = β · MW_w · g_C · VPD / P_atm`, carries total pressure explicitly ([Boscheri et al. 2012](https://simoc.space/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/Boscheri-et-al-2012.pdf)), so the higher water loss of a hypobaric greenhouse falls out of the equation instead of needing a special case. Over a five-month Arizona Lunar Greenhouse run, MEC's biomass was off by only −0.7% and water by +0.3%. Shorter windows erred by 31% (biomass), 48% (water) and 51% (condensate), and the model showed **no sensitivity to air temperature** ([Boscheri et al. 2012](https://simoc.space/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/Boscheri-et-al-2012.pdf)). A 2026 evaluation found all four Energy Cascade variants overestimated lettuce biomass (RMSE 49.58–96.99 g/m²) and underestimated transpiration, blaming "erroneous canopy closure methodology and lack of temperature responses" ([Coon et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/insilicoplants/diag011)). The three MEC versions in circulation also disagree with each other ([ASHS 2024](https://ashs2024.sched.com/event/1dtbx/ce-4-global-sensitivity-analysis-and-validation-of-the-modified-energy-cascade-crop-model-for-controlled-environment-agricult)). The verdict is that MEC is the right ledger and the wrong growth engine. Gearbox should use it for O2, CO2, water and nutrient bookkeeping at zone level, and something else for how each plant grows.

### A temperature-aware core grows each plant

For lettuce, the standard small model is **Van Henten's 1994 two-state model**: structural and non-structural dry weight, driven by canopy temperature, CO2 and PAR, with rates in s⁻¹ that work at any sub-hour step. A 2025 restatement publishes the full parameter set ([DYCOPS 2025](https://skoge.folk.ntnu.no/prost/proceedings/dycops-2025/DYCOPS2025_offline_program/contents/papers/0088.pdf)), but its input labels appear swapped and its CO2 compensation constant has an implausible unit, so it needs checking against the original before coding. The same model drives Wageningen's 2026 LettuceVisSim at a 5-minute Euler step, with shoot dry weight fitted at R² = 0.84 ([LettuceVisSim, arXiv 2609.12505](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12505v1)).

For potato, wheat and tomato, LINTUL3 gives the cleanest crop-agnostic core. Intercepted light is `PARINT = 0.5 · DTR · (1 − exp(−K·LAI))`, growth is `LUE · PARINT · min(TRANRF, NRF)` with water and nitrogen stress factors, and development runs on thermal time `max(0, T − T_base)` ([PCSE lintul3.py](https://github.com/ajwdewit/pcse/blob/HEAD/pcse/crop/lintul3.py)). The spring-wheat test set uses LUE 2.8 g/MJ and K 0.6 ([PCSE crop file](https://github.com/ajwdewit/pcse/blob/HEAD/pcse/tests/test_data/lintul3_springwheat.crop)). Enriched CO2 needs a clamp: WOFOST's generic C3 table raises maximum assimilation 1.6× at 720 ppm and saturates at 1.9× from 1000 ppm up ([WOFOST potato.yaml](https://github.com/ajwdewit/WOFOST_crop_parameters/blob/wofost81/potato.yaml)). Potato needs tuber initiation gated by temperature and photoperiod, as SUBSTOR-Potato does ([DSSAT SUBSTOR](https://dssat.net/substor-potato)), with the day length taken from the lamp schedule rather than the planet. A per-plant update costs one exponential, a few multiplies and a table lookup, so 10⁴ plants come to roughly 10⁵ operations per growth step, which is nothing next to rendering.

Nothing in Rust can be reused. crates.io has no port of WOFOST, LINTUL, Van Henten or MEC. The only crop-model crates are an AGPL Cotton2K port with 28 downloads and `vanaspati`, a single GPL-3.0-only release ([crates.io](https://crates.io/api/v1/crates?q=wofost); [vanaspati](https://github.com/MacCracken/vanaspati)). Both are copyleft and unsuitable as dependencies. Writing the models directly is the established-practice answer here, since every published plant model uses a fixed-step explicit integrator: PCSE steps daily with Euler, LettuceVisSim every 5 minutes, and the organ-resolved lettuce model with RK4 at 300 s ([PCSE](https://pcse.readthedocs.io/en/stable/reference_guide.html); [arXiv 2609.27118](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.27118v1)). A structure-of-arrays loop over ECS columns does the job, and `ode_solvers` or `diffsol` are useful only for offline verification ([ode-solvers](https://github.com/srenevey/ode-solvers); [diffsol](https://github.com/martinjrobins/diffsol)). Reference trajectories can come from DSSAT, which is BSD-3 ([DSSAT](https://dssat.net/development/open-source/)), or PCSE under EUPL ([PCSE](https://github.com/ajwdewit/pcse)). APSIM's licence forbids use without agreement ([ApsimX](https://github.com/APSIMInitiative/ApsimX)).

### Geometry is picked from a library, not grown each frame

Every production growth system builds the mature structure once and reveals it by age. SpeedTree Cinema and Houdini's L-system node both do this, Houdini with fractional generations ([SpeedTree](https://docs.speedtree.com/doku.php?id=growth); [SideFX](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/lsystem.html)). In Bevy, `bevy_flora` measures the cost of the alternative: rebuilding meshes on the CPU takes 0.1–0.4 ms per tree per frame, and growing the mature tree in the vertex shader instead cut 200 oaks from **17.6 ms to 0.81 ms**. It targets Bevy 0.20, though, was created on 10 October 2026, shipped 46 versions in four days and calls itself "Vibe Coded", so it is a design reference, not a dependency ([bevy_flora](https://github.com/Ladvien/bevy_flora)). `bevy_symbios` is the maintained L-system option on Bevy 0.19 (MIT, ABOP-compliant), but its collider bridge targets Avian3D, which gearbox does not use ([bevy_symbios](https://github.com/TheJanusStream/bevy_symbios)).

The pattern that fits gearbox is LettuceVisSim's. It uses 31 Blender prefab lettuces, one per leaf count from 3 to 34, adds one leaf per day, and scales each plant uniformly by `r = √(PPA/π)`, where projected plant area comes from dry weight through a published piecewise cubic ([LettuceVisSim](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12505v1)). That is the same convention gearbox already follows for detailed plants, packed prefab meshes rather than shader-built geometry. Bevy auto-instances entities that share a mesh and material ([Bevy instancing example](https://bevy.org/examples-webgpu/shaders/custom-shader-instancing/)) and has rendered most 3D meshes GPU-driven since 0.16 ([Bevy 0.16](https://bevy.org/news/bevy-0-16/)). One mesh per species × stage bucket, with scale, colour and droop as per-instance parameters, renders thousands of crops cheaply. The fidelity ceiling is Auburn's organ-resolved lettuce in Isaac Sim, which ray-traces light onto each leaf and reached 9.5% relative RMSE on dry weight. It simulates only 5 to 40 plants and rebuilds geometry every 6 sim-hours ([arXiv 2609.27118](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.27118v1)), so it suits a hero plant, not a farm.

### Growth runs on its own clock, not the physics clock

Bevy offers `Time<Virtual>` with `set_relative_speed`, `pause` and `max_delta`, driving `Time<Fixed>` at 64 Hz by default ([docs.rs Virtual](https://docs.rs/bevy/latest/bevy/time/struct.Virtual.html); [docs.rs Fixed](https://docs.rs/bevy/latest/bevy/time/struct.Fixed.html)). Gearbox instead runs its own 120 Hz physics accumulator, capped at 12 steps per frame ([world.rs:90-122](../bin/gearbox/src/physics/world.rs)), which limits any physics speed-up to about 6× at 60 fps. Plants live on hours and sols, so the growth clock must be separate. It should advance from `PhysicsWorld.simulated_seconds` multiplied by a growth factor, so that pausing the sim pauses plants and robots and plants share one timeline. A "fast-forward N sols" command then advances only plant, climate and sun state, in hourly substeps capped per frame, while physics stays paused. When a robot is handling plants in real time, the step drops to 5 minutes, the LettuceVisSim rate. Climate inputs are computed once per tray or zone, not once per plant.

### Stress must show, and must never surprise

Robot-facing observables fall out of the state in closed form. Canopy cover is `1 − exp(−k·LAI)`. NDVI follows Baret and Guyot's saturating form `VI = VI∞ + (VIg − VI∞)·exp(−K_VI·LAI)` ([HAL](https://hal.inrae.fr/hal-02701349)). Diameter comes from projected area, and fresh mass is dry mass divided by (1 − water fraction). Stress has ready-made drivers too. LINTUL3's water factor TRANRF and nitrogen index NNI cut light-use efficiency and leaf area and speed up leaf death ([PCSE lintul3.py](https://github.com/ajwdewit/pcse/blob/HEAD/pcse/crop/lintul3.py)), and off-nominal humidity roughly halved MEC's calibrated lettuce efficiency from 0.007–0.017 to 0.003–0.010 ([Amitrano et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7308940/)). On screen, a fast water-status value drives leaf droop in the vertex shader, as a cheap stand-in for SIGGRAPH Asia 2023's water-to-stiffness wilting model ([Maggioli et al. 2023](https://gladia.di.uniroma1.it/publication/maggioli-2023-plant/)). A chlorophyll proxy from NNI lerps leaf albedo toward yellow, oldest leaves first, following PROSPECT's chlorophyll-to-reflectance physics ([PROSPECT-MP](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5526878)). The same value lowers simulated NDVI, so camera colour and sensor readings agree.

Games show what to avoid. Surviving Mars reduces potatoes to a 4-sol timer that uses 2.4 water and yields 66 food ([Surviving Mars wiki](https://survivingmars.paradoxwikis.com/Crops)). Occupy Mars players lost whole domes to a brief supply glitch, and the developer explained the cause was an invisible temperature drop ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/758690/discussions/0/4032473196483596877)). Space Engineers' Event Controller cannot even detect a ripe crop ([Space Engineers wiki](https://spaceengineers.wiki.gg/wiki/Farm_Plot)). Stardew Valley's gentler rule, that an unwatered day means no growth rather than death ([Stardew wiki](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Crops)), is the better model. For a robot simulator the rule is stricter still: decline is gradual, and every plant state a robot is expected to act on must be observable through a sensor. No shipped game models light quantity, spectrum or plant shape, which leaves that ground open for gearbox.

## A 5.7-metre container and a ceiling-rail arm are the realistic template

### EDEN ISS is the only space greenhouse documented to the millimetre

DLR's EDEN ISS is two 20 ft high-cube containers. A cold-porch airlock opens into a service section (subsystems, sink, workbench), and a sealed door leads from there into the Future Exploration Greenhouse, whose interior is **5.7 × 2.15 × 2.55 m (31.5 m³) with about 12.5 m² of cultivation area** ([Zabel et al. 2017](https://elib.dlr.de/114876/1/Zabel%20et%20al.%20FEG%20Design%20ICES%202017_v3.pdf)). The paper also gives every interior detail. A 1.0 m centre aisle lets two or three people work; a side aisle was rejected because racks 1150 mm deep would be too deep to reach. Four racks stand on each side, 2250 mm tall with 575 mm shelves, built from 30 × 30 mm aluminium profile with one, two or four levels. The racks hold 42 dark-grey 600 × 400 mm Euroboxes with crop-specific lids, rock-wool plugs, root misters and a nutrient-film runoff. Lighting is 42 water-cooled Heliospectra lamps with red 630 nm, blue 450 nm, far-red 735 nm and white 5700 K channels, giving up to 300 or 600 µmol/m²/s and cutting out above 50 °C. Air arrives at 1400 m³/h through eight tapered ducts at 0.4 m/s over the canopy. Two nutrient loops run at pH 5.8 and EC 2.5 or 3.5 mS/cm, white laminate covers the walls, and a stainless leak basin and a 300 mm sub-floor sit under metal grating. Twenty-one top-view and eight side-view cameras photograph the trays daily ([Zabel et al. 2017](https://elib.dlr.de/114876/1/Zabel%20et%20al.%20FEG%20Design%20ICES%202017_v3.pdf)). Each tray maps directly to sim state: PPFD, spectrum, EC, pH, root-zone wetness and local air speed. The facility is now being rebuilt as EDEN LUNA in Cologne, with a project term of 2024 to 2026 and a robot arm among the planned upgrades ([DLR EDEN LUNA](https://www.dlr.de/en/research-and-transfer/projects-and-missions/eden-luna)).

### On Mars the box goes underground or under ice

NASA expects the Arizona prototype cylinders (2.06 m in diameter by 5.5 m, 11 m² of canopy each, [UA CEAC](https://ceac.arizona.edu/about/facilities)) would "probably be buried under regolith for radiation protection", lit by LEDs or by sunlight piped through fibre optics ([NASA 2017](https://www.nasa.gov/science-research/lunar-martian-greenhouses-designed-to-mimic-those-on-earth)). NASA Langley's Mars Ice Home puts at least 3 m of water ice over a 198 m² torus, shielding cosmic rays while still letting daylight in, and assigns its upper floor to growing food ([Ice Home ConOps](http://bigidea.nianet.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/IceDome-ConOps-2017-12-21v-reduced.pdf)). AI SpaceFactory's MARSHA puts a hydroponic garden on its third level ([livinspaces](https://livinspaces.net/ls-tv/ai-spacefactory-use-3d-printing-technology-to-develop-marsha-a-possible-habitat-for-mars/)), and NASA's Mars Dune Alpha planned four aeroponic tray towers by the kitchen ([ASCE](https://asce.org/publications-and-news/civil-engineering-source/civil-engineering-magazine/issues/magazine-issue/article/2023/09/a-3d-printed-habitat-is-home-for-yearlong-missions-to-mars)). Low pressure also changes the hardware: with little convective cooling, lamps and heaters risk overheating ([Univ. Saskatchewan URJ](https://usurj.journals.usask.ca/article/view/265)). The realistic first Mars greenhouse is therefore an opaque, regolith-bermed, LED-lit EDEN-style module, which happens to be the cheapest kind for gearbox to render and sense. The glazed dome of the film and of Surviving Mars is the cinematic variant. It costs more in gearbox and is harder to defend against 1000× UV and a tau-10 storm.

### Three robot shapes fit, and none of them is fast

No robot has yet tended crops in an operational space greenhouse. The most concrete design is DLR's **EVE**: a 7-DOF arm from DLR's lightweight manipulator line on a ceiling-mounted, L-shaped Beckhoff linear rail that parks the arm in a stowage bay. It carries a soft three-finger CLASH hand and a wrist-mounted RealSense RGB-D camera, and runs under shared autonomy, with hierarchical state machines composing skills such as drive mover, move arm, identify object and grasp object ([Prince et al., IAC-24](https://elib.dlr.de/210298/1/IAC24_manuscript_Autonomous_Robotic_System_Plant_Cultivation_Luna_Surface_author_version.pdf)). It draws about 700 W, has an installed mass of about 170 kg and uses an arm derived from DLR's "This Is Not an Arm" (TINA) ([Universe Today](https://www.universetoday.com/articles/can-a-greenhouse-with-a-robotic-arm-feed-the-next-lunar-astronauts)). In lab trials it took **23.46 s per tomato with 58.3% success and a 36.1% drop rate**. Failures came from leaves hiding fruit, fruit swinging away from the fingers and a perception model reused from bin-picking. DLR budgets 9.07 minutes for a 0.783 m² tray, including a 5 m rail traverse at 5 cm/s ([Prince et al., IAC-24](https://elib.dlr.de/210298/1/IAC24_manuscript_Autonomous_Robotic_System_Plant_Cultivation_Luna_Surface_author_version.pdf)). The same paper's survey of agricultural harvesters averages 19.3 s per cycle and 64.4% success. A NASA Kennedy and Lockheed Martin prototype adds a pneumatic tool changer with a latch tool for opening chambers and a plant shear ([UNLV repository](https://oasis.library.unlv.edu/me_fac_articles/956/)).

Terrestrial greenhouses add two more shapes. SWEEPER rode greenhouse pipe rails with a FANUC arm and picked sweet peppers in 24 s, with 61% success under best-fit crop conditions and 18% under current ones ([Arad et al. 2020](https://research.wur.nl/en/publications/development-of-a-sweet-pepper-harvesting-robot/)). Priva's commercial Kompano de-leafs tomatoes from the same heating-pipe rails and claims over 85% effectiveness across 1 ha a week ([Priva](https://www.priva.com/blog/horticulture/kompano-automated-leaf-cutting-robot)). Iron Ox's Grover carried whole 1000 lb grow modules to fixed scanning and harvest stations ([AgroSpectrum](https://agrospectrumindia.com/news/64/26416/iron-ox-launches-autonomous-mobile-robot-grover.html)) before the company shut down ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/agtech-foodtech/vertical-farming-venture-capital-plenty-appharvest/)), part of a wave that included Plenty's Chapter 11 in March 2025 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/24/vertical-farming-company-plenty-files-for-bankruptcy-after-raising-nearly-1b)). The three archetypes map onto layouts. An overhead rail arm suits a narrow 1 m aisle where a floor robot would block the crew, an aisle cart suits long rows of vine crops, and a tray lifter suits an open floor that brings plants to a station. In gearbox, all three are prismatic and revolute joints in a USD file.

### Maintenance, not harvest, fills the robot's day

EDEN ISS measured where the hours go. Its task catalogue has four clusters: crop cultivation (sowing, thinning, pruning, pollination, transplanting, harvest, separating inedible biomass), maintenance (filters, cleaning, nutrient preparation, sensor calibration, tanks, consumables), repair, and science sampling ([Zabel et al. 2019](https://elib.dlr.de/128530/1/Zabel_EDEN%20ISS%20crew%20time_v2.pdf)). Sowing a lettuce tray took about 1:35 min, harvesting 1:18 and clearing inedible biomass 2:54, while pruning a dwarf-tomato tray took about 16 minutes. Cycles ran 37.7 days for lettuce, 23.3 for radish, 161 for cucumber and 286 for dwarf tomato. **Maintenance took 174 hours a season, more than plant tending**, and the nutrient tanks had to be drained, cleaned and refilled seven times because of biofilm and calcium and potassium deficiency symptoms ([Zabel et al. 2019](https://elib.dlr.de/128530/1/Zabel_EDEN%20ISS%20crew%20time_v2.pdf)). At NASA's estimate of about $130,000 per crew hour, the 11.5 min/day/m² EDEN needed is what justifies robots at all ([Prince et al., IAC-24](https://elib.dlr.de/210298/1/IAC24_manuscript_Autonomous_Robotic_System_Plant_Cultivation_Luna_Surface_author_version.pdf)).

The documented failures read like a scenario list. EDEN ISS had a cooling-loop leak and a failed fan ([EDEN ISS blog](https://eden-iss.net/index.php/2018/06/04/analogue-mission-antarctica-several-months-in-and-running/)). Low airflow on ISS Veggie let free water collect on zinnias, which then caught *Fusarium* ([Schuerger et al. 2021](https://plantpath.ifas.ufl.edu/misc/media/Schuerger_2021_Astrobiology_F_oxy.pdf)). Lunar Palace's simulated **power outages of 2–4 h raised CO2 by 251–312 ppm per hour**, and the air took 12–24 h to restabilise ([bioRxiv 2021](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.01.12.426282.full.pdf)). An electrical heater burned down the MDRS GreenHab ([Space.com](https://www.space.com/28388-greenhouse-fire-mock-mars-mission.html)). Logistics add the outside: Mars Ice Home sizes a cargo airlock at 3 × 2.7 × 2.2 m and 899 kg for an MSL-class teleoperated rover ([Ice Home ConOps](http://bigidea.nianet.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/IceDome-ConOps-2017-12-21v-reduced.pdf)), and suitports are the leading answer to abrasive dust ([Jones, SAE 2007-01-3210](https://saemobilus.sae.org/papers/planetary-airlock-suitlock-requirements-alternate-approaches-2007-01-3210)). A believable gearbox mission mix therefore includes valve, bottle, filter and tank manipulation, canister shuttles from an airlock and failure injections, and is not only fruit picking.

## Dust, not gas, colours the Mars sky, and Bevy already ships the physics

### The outdoor sky needs Mie dust with a chromatic phase function

Mars's sky colour comes from suspended iron-bearing dust about 1–2 µm across ([Lemmon et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.4234)). It is butterscotch by day and blue only in a tight glow around the sun, strongest at sunset, as Curiosity's sol-956 sunset showed ([NASA PIA19400](https://pds-rings.seti.org/press_releases/pages/PIA19xxx/PIA19400.html)). Mie modelling shows that glow comes from strong forward scattering of blue light by the dust, which no Rayleigh-style model can reproduce ([Ehlers et al., WUSTL](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/blue-moons-and-martian-sunsets/)). Sky chromaticity runs from dark yellowish-brown at high opacity to bluish-black when dust-free ([Bell et al. 2006](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/chromaticity-of-the-martian-sky-as-observed-by-the-mars-explorati)). A Monte Carlo sky model uses dust single-scattering albedo of 0.922, 0.797 and 0.690 at 615, 551 and 450 nm ([Albers](https://stevealbers.net/albers/allsky/mars.html)). The best real-time model, Schneegans et al. 2024, uses feldspar dust with 3% hematite: without the hematite the sky turns grey, and above 6% it turns unrealistically red ([Schneegans et al. 2024](https://cgvr.cs.uni-bremen.de/papers/cgf24/v43i2_15_15010.pdf)).

Gearbox uses the wrong mechanism today. `PlanetConfig::barren` takes Earth's medium and swaps the Rayleigh channels to (18e-6, 10e-6, 5e-6) ([config.rs](../crates/gearbox-planet/src/config.rs)), and `bevy_weather`'s `SkyKind::Dust` paints a hand-tuned zenith and horizon palette ([daylight.rs:40-59](../crates/bevy_weather/src/daylight.rs)). The docs call the result "visual approximations, not calibrated radiometry" ([PLANET_WORLDS.md](../docs/PLANET_WORLDS.md)). A reddened Rayleigh sky looks red but can never produce the blue aureole. The fix is already in the dependency tree. **Bevy 0.19.1 ships `ScatteringMedium::mars` and `Atmosphere::mars`** with the Schneegans coefficients: dust scattering (30.67, 25.39, 20.93)×10⁻⁶ m⁻¹, a 4 km and 20 km double-exponential profile, a chromatic phase texture, a 3,389.5 km inner radius and ground albedo 0.1 ([docs.rs bevy_light 0.19.1](https://docs.rs/bevy_light/0.19.1/src/bevy_light/atmosphere.rs.html); [Bevy PR #22884](https://code.morphllm.com/bevyengine/bevy/commit/acd1091c3ee5bc66ed9ae6f2ef13eca82fcedd89)). The phase texture `mars_mie_phase.ktx2` lives in Bevy's repo assets rather than the crate, so gearbox must copy it ([Bevy atmosphere example](https://bevy.org/examples/3d-rendering/atmosphere/)); its licence was not verified. The Mars medium should replace the barren medium and the Dust palette outright, not sit beside them as a mode.

Three numbers need care. Bevy 0.19.1 has no `SunDisk::MARS` ([docs.rs directional_light.rs](https://docs.rs/bevy_light/0.19.1/src/bevy_light/directional_light.rs.html)); the sun's diameter over Mars's mean distance gives an angular size of about 0.00611 rad. The irradiance ratio of 0.431 ([NASA NSSDC](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/marsfact.html)) puts mean top-of-atmosphere sunlight near 56,000 lux. Gearbox's 58,000 lux and EV100 13 are already close ([environment.rs:9-35](../bin/gearbox/src/environment.rs)), better than Bevy's own example, which reuses Earth's 130,000 lux on Mars. Integrating the preset's dust profile gives a vertical tau of about 0.146, a clear day, so a `dust_tau` knob maps to a density multiplier of roughly tau/0.146, about 3.4× at tau 0.5 and 7× at tau 1. Bevy's ground albedo of 0.1 is too dark for a dusty site: measured scene albedos run 0.11–0.30 ([AGU FM15](https://agu.confex.com/agu/fm15/webprogram/Paper81778.html)), with dust-devil tracks near 0.20 and dusty deposits near 0.30 ([arXiv 1408.5301](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.5301)). Using 0.2–0.3, with `AtmosphereEnvironmentMapLight` carrying the butterscotch cast into ambient light ([docs.rs bevy_pbr atmosphere](https://docs.rs/bevy_pbr/0.19.1/src/bevy_pbr/atmosphere/mod.rs.html)), completes the clear-day sky.

### Storms, dust devils and frost are separate effects layered on top

Precomputed-LUT skies are fitted to thin atmospheres, and Schneegans et al. flag artefacts for dense ones and warn that the strong forward peak gives a very high dynamic range ([Schneegans et al. 2024](https://cgvr.cs.uni-bremen.de/papers/cgf24/v43i2_15_15010.pdf)). A tau-5-to-11 storm therefore needs Bevy's raymarched atmosphere mode plus a near-ground `FogVolume` or `VolumetricFog` in dust colour ([docs.rs volumetric.rs](https://docs.rs/bevy_light/0.19.1/src/bevy_light/volumetric.rs.html)), with the direct sun cut by Beer–Lambert `exp(−tau/cos z)` and the diffuse share raised. The same tau must feed the greenhouse light ledger, so a storm darkens the sky and stalls sunlit crops at the same moment. Dust devils belong outside the atmosphere model as instanced volumetric columns. Perseverance filmed one about **65 m wide and at least 400 m tall moving about 1 m/s** ([NASA/JPL PIA26528](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/images/pia26528-perserverance-captures-crater-rim-dust-devil-eating-its-own/)). Frost is a morning effect only: CaSSIS found water frost on the Tharsis calderas about 0.01 mm thick that lasts a few hours after sunrise ([Valantinas et al. 2024](https://nomad.aeronomie.be/publications/225-evidence-for-transient-morning-water-frost-deposits-on-the-tharsis-volcanoes-of-mars)).

Real terrain is free. The NASA/USGS HiRISE DTMs on AWS are CC0 Cloud-Optimised GeoTIFFs ([AWS Open Data](https://registry.opendata.aws/nasa-usgs-mars-hirise-dtms/)), 1,338 sites are listed ([uahirise.org](https://www.uahirise.org/dtm/)), and a 1 m HiRISE terrain already runs in Gazebo ([arXiv 2509.11082](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.11082)). Poly Haven assets, including its Namaqualand desert scans, are CC0 ([Poly Haven FAQ](https://polyhaven.com/faq); [CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2024/10/download-poly-havens-free-namaqualand-3d-scan-library/)). NASA's 3D models are free provided NASA logos are kept off derived branding ([NASA-3D-Resources](https://github.com/nasa/NASA-3D-Resources); [NASA media guidelines](https://nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media)), and Space Robotics Bench publishes CC0 procedural assets, including Perseverance ([lib.rs srb_py](https://lib.rs/crates/srb_py)). The precedent leaves a clear opening. Space Robotics Bench has a Mars domain preset but no plants ([arXiv 2509.23328](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23328v1)), and Helios has GPU plant growth and radiation transport but only under Earth sun and lights ([Bailey 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6813926/)). No open simulator found joins a Mars sky, a pressurised greenhouse and an agricultural manipulator.

### Inside, the light is magenta and the glass gets dirty

NASA's Veggie uses 132 red, 32 green and 32 blue LEDs because, in Gioia Massa's words, plants "look gray and purple" under red and blue alone ([KQED](https://www.kqed.org/bayareabites/83133/the-salad-frontier-why-astronauts-need-to-grow-lettuce-in-space)). Signify finds that adding at least 5% green is enough for inspection ([Philips Hortiblog](https://www.lighting.philips.com/application-areas/specialist-applications/horticulture/hortiblog/light-and-growth/myth-surrounding-white-light-part-3)), and Veggie's disease outbreak was diagnosed better from white-flash photos than red/blue images ([Schuerger et al. 2021](https://plantpath.ifas.ufl.edu/misc/media/Schuerger_2021_Astrobiology_F_oxy.pdf)). In an RGB renderer this needs no special shader. A lamp coloured about (1.0, 0.0–0.05, 0.6–0.9) in linear RGB turns a green leaf a dark purple-grey, and switching on the white channel before running plant-health perception becomes a natural robot task. The interior palette is EDEN's: white laminate, dark-grey trays, bare aluminium profile and metal grating ([Zabel et al. 2017](https://elib.dlr.de/114876/1/Zabel%20et%20al.%20FEG%20Design%20ICES%202017_v3.pdf)).

For the dome variant, ETFE film transmits 85–95% ([Tensaform](https://pdf.archiexpo.com/pdf/tensaform/etfe/59245-377897.html); [BDIR](https://bdir.com/news/performance-and-characteristics-of-etfe-membrane)). Settled dust costs Mars solar arrays about 0.2% of output per sol, within a range of 0.05–2%, until wind or a dust devil cleans them ([Lorenz et al. 2021](https://digital.inta.es/entities/publication/e629d870-297b-4f08-89ce-ee637569c063/full)). One `dust` scalar per pane, rising about 0.002 per sol and reset by cleaning, can drive Bevy's `specular_transmission`, roughness and base colour ([docs.rs pbr_material.rs](https://docs.rs/bevy_pbr/0.19.1/src/bevy_pbr/pbr_material.rs.html)), weighted toward up-facing surfaces, and the same scalar scales the PAR reaching the plants. That mirrors gearbox's own rule of one wear scalar driving ground and vegetation together. usd_bevy already maps UsdPreviewSurface `transmission` to `specular_transmission` ([material.rs:420-465](/home/bresilla/data/code/OUSD/bevy_openusd/crates/usd_bevy/src/route/material.rs)) but approximates `RectLight` as a point light ([light.rs:1-13](/home/bresilla/data/code/OUSD/bevy_openusd/crates/usd_bevy/src/route/light.rs)), and gearbox fixes EV100 per planet ([environment.rs:33](../bin/gearbox/src/environment.rs)). A camera walking from Mars daylight into an LED room therefore needs its own exposure.

## Gearbox has the planet and the arms, but plants, climate and time are missing

### What exists today and where it stops

| Need | What gearbox has | What is missing |
|---|---|---|
| Mars world | `--planet mars`: 3,389.5 km radius, 3.711 m/s², procedural regolith with craters, dunes and streamed boulders | Pad flat for only ~6 m; boulder exclusion 8 m; no config file |
| Sky and sun | Hand-tuned Dust palette, fixed sun near 40°, 58 klx, EV100 13 | Mie dust, tau, storms, sol cycle; calendar is Earth's |
| Structures | World-category USD with static colliders, UsdLux lights, glass via opacity, ior and transmission | `metersPerUnit` defaults to 0.01; world loads not snapped; API world loads land only at home |
| Plants | GPU-hash vegetation (1.5 M blade records per view), seven Poly Haven packs, all zero on Mars | Identity, state, growth, bed placement, shadows; crop meshes; the packer script |
| Climate | Sun, sky light, haze and one global wind in `bevy_weather` | Temperature, humidity, CO2, O2, pressure, PAR; indoor zones |
| Time | 120 Hz physics, play, pause, step, `simulated_seconds` | `scene speed` errors; no growth clock |
| Sensing | Colour, depth, LiDAR, recognition, light, IMU, GPS and more | Depth, LiDAR and recognition see colliders only; light sensor reads only the brightest directional light |
| Acting | Revolute, prismatic, D6 and world-anchor joints (Isaac-verified), joint leases, Perseverance's arm, connectors, bale contact-harvest | Gripper, sow, prune, pick; any greenhouse robot |
| API | `SceneEvent` with a `harvested` kind, scene list, `HostBus` servers, `Props` maps | Plant and climate topics or types |

The site works but is small. Mars launches with `oslo make run --args='--planet mars --load machines/usd/perseverance_nasa.usdz'` ([PLANET_WORLDS.md:9-17](../docs/PLANET_WORLDS.md)) and sets Mars's radius and gravity from a hard-coded body ([body.rs:43-57](../crates/gearbox-globe/src/body.rs)). Terrain is level only within about 6 m of home, reaching full relief by about 22 m ([terrain.rs:46-50](../crates/gearbox-globe/src/terrain.rs)), and boulders are skipped only within 8 m ([planet_rocks.rs:215-217](../bin/gearbox/src/planet_rocks.rs)). An EDEN-length container barely fits, and a multi-module outpost does not. A greenhouse must load as a world-category USD. Loading it as a "static" prop gives it a dynamic capsule body that settles and freezes ([world.rs:906-958](../bin/gearbox/src/world.rs)). The stage must author `metersPerUnit = 1` because the default is 0.01 ([MACHINE_SPEC.md:34-41](../specs/MACHINE_SPEC.md)), and its height must be authored, since world loads are not terrain-snapped ([world.rs:733-757](../bin/gearbox/src/world.rs)). It should not author a `PhysicsScene`, because the machine spec and the planet docs disagree about which gravity wins ([MACHINE_SPEC.md:44-46](../specs/MACHINE_SPEC.md); [PLANET_WORLDS.md](../docs/PLANET_WORLDS.md)).

The vegetation system cannot become a crop system. Each clump instance derives its position, yaw and scale from a hash of its index and chunk on every frame ([clumps.wgsl:143-210](../crates/gearbox-fields/src/clumps/shaders/clumps.wgsl)). `VegetationLayer` has no growth or per-instance field ([profile.rs:49-80](../crates/gearbox-fields/src/profile.rs)), plants stand only on the procedural terrain heightmap ([host.rs:8-26](../crates/gearbox-fields/src/host.rs)), one global wind leans them indoors and out ([host.rs:36-57](../crates/gearbox-fields/src/host.rs)), and nothing in the pipeline casts a shadow ([render.rs:198](../crates/gearbox-fields/src/render.rs)). The Poly Haven packer `scripts/pack_vegetation.py` is gone from the working tree, last touched in commit 9d7acbc. The decision follows: greenhouse crops should be ordinary Bevy entities with a `Plant` component and shared stage-bucket meshes, numbering thousands rather than millions, while `gearbox-fields` stays the outdoor ground-cover renderer. Plants need identity, bed-top placement and colliders, and the hash pipeline offers none of them.

Time and climate are absent. The CLI's `scene speed` answers "the sim has no time scale yet" ([scene.rs:76-78](../crates/gearbox-cli/src/cli/scene.rs)). The sun calendar is an Earth NOAA formula that only UI sliders move ([calendar.rs:3-40](../crates/bevy_weather/src/calendar.rs)), and on Mars the sun is a fixed override ([environment.rs:24-27](../bin/gearbox/src/environment.rs)). `WeatherSettings` holds no temperature, humidity, CO2 or pressure ([lib.rs:26-50](../crates/bevy_weather/src/lib.rs)).

Sensing is the sharpest gap. Depth always comes from a Molla trace of the collision scene ([links.rs:287-313](../bin/gearbox/src/links.rs)), recognition labels physics shapes ([sensors.rs:758-797](../bin/gearbox/src/sensors.rs)), and lean sensor cameras exclude vegetation ([sensor_cameras.rs:153-162](../bin/gearbox/src/sensor_cameras.rs)). A GPU-drawn plant is therefore invisible to every robot sensor except a full colour render. The light sensor takes only the brightest directional light as the sun ([sensor_generic.rs:376-392](../bin/gearbox/src/sensor_generic.rs)), so grow lamps do not register. Glazing built as colliders would block LiDAR and light rays. It needs `physics:collisionEnabled = false` ([MACHINE_SPEC.md:271](../specs/MACHINE_SPEC.md)) or a sensor-transparent flag that does not exist yet.

Acting is the strongest area. Revolute, prismatic, D6 and world-anchored joints with position control are conformance-tested against Isaac ([ROBOT_CONTRACT.md:423-453](../specs/ROBOT_CONTRACT.md)), so an EVE-style ceiling rail plus arm, or an XYZ gantry, is plain USD. Connectors can lock an end effector to a peer body ([backend.rs:838-865](../bin/gearbox/src/physics/backend.rs)). `harvest_bales_on_machine_contact` already despawns a prop when a machine body touches it and publishes a `harvested` event ([world.rs:1002-1058](../bin/gearbox/src/world.rs)), and `HostBus` shows how to add a new topic server ([host.rs:94-150](../crates/gearbox-api/src/host.rs)). Picking ripe fruit generalises that bale rule.

### The build order follows the data dependencies

| Step | Build | Needs | Done when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mars time and sky: sol calendar (24.6597 h day, solar longitude, 25.19° obliquity) driving the sun; growth clock with factor and fast-forward; `ScatteringMedium::mars` replacing the barren medium and Dust palette; `dust_tau` knob | nothing | Sun rises and sets every sol; tau 0.15, 1 and 10 look distinct; plant clock skips 30 sols in seconds |
| 2 | Site and shell: flat pad and boulder exclusion taken from loaded structure footprints; EDEN-dimension world USD (cold porch, service room, greenhouse) bermed in regolith; indoor zone with zero wind | 1 for look-dev | A rover drives from the dock through the airlock into the aisle with no rocks or relief indoors |
| 3 | Climate and light ledger: per-zone temperature, humidity, CO2, O2, pressure and PAR; lamp entities whose authored PPFD drives both the Bevy light and plant PAR; MEC gas and water ledger | 1, 2 | A 3 h lamp and fan outage raises CO2 at roughly the 251–312 ppm/h Lunar Palace measured |
| 4 | Plant state and growth: `Plant` entities with species files from BVAD; Van Henten lettuce, LINTUL core for the rest; stress factors | 3 | Lettuce at 17 mol/m²/d harvests in 28–38 days with yields inside the BVAD and EDEN band |
| 5 | Plant rendering: packed stage-bucket meshes per species on tray tops; per-instance scale, colour and droop; white inspection channel | 4 | A time-lapse shows lettuce adding leaves; chlorosis is visible under white light and hidden under magenta |
| 6 | Plant sensing: collider proxies per plant and fruit labelled by id and refreshed only on stage changes; a PAR and climate sensor kind | 4, 5 | Depth, LiDAR and recognition return plant ids |
| 7 | Plant API and actions: plants list, stage and harvest events, sow and harvest requests; contact harvest generalised from bales; fruit bodies with connectors; EVE-style ceiling-rail arm | 6 | A scripted client harvests a tray, and leaf occlusion pulls success below 100% |
| 8 | Scenarios: dust storm, power loss, fan failure leading to mould, leak into the basin, tank biofilm, regolith bed gated by perchlorate | 3–7 | Each failure is injectable over the API and recoverable by robot action |

The order puts time and light first because every later system consumes them. Without a sol cycle and a tau value there is nothing to grow on and nothing to look-dev the shell under. The climate ledger comes before plants because plants are functions of climate, and building it first lets the power-cut test validate the ledger before any plant exists. Rendering comes after state so the meshes show the model rather than a timer. Colliders follow rendering because their shapes come from the same stage buckets, and they should change only on stage transitions, since the sensor rig re-snapshots the scene on every revision ([sensors.rs:1-8](../bin/gearbox/src/sensors.rs)). Robots come late because gearbox's joints, leases and contact events already work, and an arm with nothing to pick tests nothing.

Several unknowns could reorder the later steps. Nobody has measured how many static colliders Molla's sensor snapshot and contact solver tolerate, which decides whether every lettuce gets a proxy or only every tray. Bevy transmission and shadows through glass inside the mara viewport are unverified, which is one more reason to build the opaque module first. The Van Henten parameters and the MEC canopy-closure polynomials must be checked against primary sources. The science has open holes too. The research found no long seed-to-seed crop trial below 10 kPa and no data on plant responses to 0.38 g or a 24.66 h day. Nor did it find a peer-reviewed table of surface PAR against tau and sun angle; the one paper that estimated global surface PAR for greenhouse siting is available only as an abstract ([SAE 2005-01-2813](https://saemobilus.sae.org/papers/global-estimates-photosynthetically-active-radiation-mars-surface-2005-01-2813)). The sim should expose those as tunable assumptions, not hide them as constants.

## Conclusion

Realism and engineering cost point the same way here, which is unusual. The most defensible Mars greenhouse is an opaque, regolith-bermed, LED-lit hydroponic box, and it is also the version that sidesteps gearbox's riskiest unknowns: glass shadows, transmission in the viewport, sensor rays through glazing and the jump in exposure from daylight to grow light. The glass dome of the film is both less realistic and more expensive to build, so it belongs late, as a variant. The deeper payoff of using MEC is coupling, not growth curves. Once plants exchange O2, CO2 and water with a zone ledger, power cuts, fan failures and dust storms produce consequences without being scripted, and EDEN ISS's finding that maintenance outweighs harvesting turns the robot's job into valves, tanks and filters as much as fruit.

The field has a genuine gap. Robotics benches have Mars without plants, and agricultural simulators have plants under Earth's sun. A gearbox that grows BVAD-calibrated lettuce under a Mie-dust sky and lets an EVE-like arm fail at realistic rates would be the first open simulator of its kind. That honesty is the hard requirement. If the sim omits leaf occlusion, swinging fruit and purple light, its harvest success will read near 100%, against the 58% that DLR's real arm achieved, and any controller tuned in it will fail on real hardware.
